Jigawa State Unveils Comprehensive Data on Education, Agriculture, and Economy

Headline: Jigawa unveils new data on education, agriculture and economic performance (Premium Times)

What happened: The Jigawa State Government, led by Governor Umar Namadi, unveiled four major statistical reports on Tuesday at the Banquet Hall, Government House, Dutse. These reports include the 2024/2025 Annual School Census, the Non-Formal Education Census, the Agricultural Structure Survey, and the Jigawa State Gross Domestic Product (GDP) Survey 2019–2025. Governor Namadi emphasized the crucial role of reliable data in guiding development priorities, efficiently allocating resources, and measuring progress across key sectors. The Annual School Census recorded 1,320,770 pupils in public and private primary schools, with girls constituting 57% (752,921), but highlighted high pupil-teacher ratios (96:1 in public primary schools). The Non-Formal Education Census identified 10,197 centers catering to 1,116,289 learners, revealing that 66% of Almajiri learners depend on community support for meals and 61.1% of sleeping facilities require major repairs. The Agricultural Structure Survey found 34.3% of farming households experienced severe food insecurity, with limited access to extension services (7.1%) and credit (3.9%). The GDP Survey showed the state's nominal GDP increased by 35.92%, from ₦3.62 trillion in 2019 to ₦5.65 trillion in 2023.

Deep Lore / Context: This comprehensive data unveiling is a significant step forward in the "National Progress & Economy" storyline, particularly in the realm of evidence-based governance. It directly addresses the challenge of data scarcity and reliability that Finance Minister Taiwo Oyedele recently acknowledged, stating that "it's difficult to find data about Nigeria, especially from the fiscal side." By systematically collecting and publishing detailed statistics across critical sectors, Jigawa State is setting a precedent for improved transparency and accountability, which is vital for effective policy formulation and resource allocation. This initiative connects to the broader efforts to strengthen Nigeria's statistical system and improve public-sector decision-making, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to concrete figures.

The findings themselves paint a mixed picture of progress and persistent challenges. While the increase in nominal GDP is positive, the high pupil-teacher ratios, the welfare issues of Almajiri learners, and the widespread food insecurity underscore the deep-seated development gaps that require urgent attention. The limited access to agricultural support services also highlights structural impediments to boosting productivity in a sector that employs a large portion of the population. Governor Namadi's directive for the Bureau of Statistics to integrate these findings into sector performance reviews, medium-term strategies, and the state budget cycle is crucial. Headline: Don’t restore fuel subsidy, IMF tells FG (Daily Trust) Headline: Rising debt: IMF urges FG to boost revenue, cut waste (Vanguard)

What happened: The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has reiterated its advice to the Federal Government against restoring broad petrol subsidies. During a press briefing on the fund’s fiscal monitor at the 2026 annual meetings of the IMF and World Bank in Bangkok, Thailand, Rodrigo Valdés, director of the IMF’s fiscal affairs department, argued that blanket petrol subsidies are fiscally very costly, often regressive, disproportionately benefiting higher-income consumers, and can worsen fiscal pressures amidst rising borrowing costs. He stated that fuel subsidies cost countries between 1% and 1.5% of GDP in 2026. Instead, the IMF recommended deploying targeted support for vulnerable households through social safety nets and household data to cushion the impact of rising energy costs. This advice comes as the FG recently announced a 30-day petrol discount at NNPC Limited retail stations, prioritizing public transport operators, to mitigate the impact of rising fuel prices. Separately, the IMF also urged the FG to boost revenue and cut waste to address rising debt.

Deep Lore / Context: This is a direct continuation and reinforcement of the "CBN Policy and Economic Management" and "National Progress & Economy" storylines, which have seen ongoing debates around the removal of fuel subsidies and broader fiscal reforms. The IMF's consistent stance against broad subsidies highlights the international community's pressure on Nigeria to adopt more sustainable fiscal policies. The argument that subsidies are regressive and costly aligns with the government's initial rationale for removal, but the socio-economic impact has been severe, leading to calls for their restoration, especially ahead of an election year. This creates a delicate balancing act for the government: maintaining fiscal discipline as advised by international bodies versus addressing public outcry and the cost of living crisis.

The IMF's recommendation for targeted support through social safety nets is a critical alternative, but its effective implementation in Nigeria has historically been challenging due to issues of data accuracy, corruption, and logistical hurdles in reaching the truly vulnerable.
