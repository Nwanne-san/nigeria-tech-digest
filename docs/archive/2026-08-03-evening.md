### State Police Bill Ready for Tinubu’s Review Sept 3 — Gbajabiamila (Daily Trust)

**What happened:** The Presidential Working Group on the National Policing Bill, led by Femi Gbajabiamila, announced that the proposed state police executive bill will be presented to President Bola Tinubu for review on September 3, 2026. This date is part of a seven-week timetable set for developing the legal framework for state police. Gbajabiamila made this disclosure after the inaugural meeting of the working group, signaling a concrete step towards a long-debated security reform.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This development marks a significant escalation in the ongoing storyline of "Nigeria's Infrastructure for Insecurity Response," which has been tracking the persistent and escalating insecurity across the nation. For years, the call for state police has been a central point of debate, with proponents arguing it would allow for more localized and effective responses to banditry, kidnappings, and other forms of violence that have overwhelmed the federal police force. Opponents, however, have raised concerns about potential abuse by state governors, funding challenges, and the risk of further politicization of security apparatuses. The current administration, facing immense pressure from widespread insecurity, including recent massacres and abductions, has seemingly thrown its weight behind the idea. The establishment of a dedicated working group and a clear timeline for the bill's review indicates a serious commitment to this structural reform. The September 3 deadline suggests an accelerated push, reflecting the urgency of the security situation and the public's demand for tangible solutions beyond intensified military operations. This move could fundamentally alter Nigeria's security architecture, decentralizing power and potentially empowering states to better protect their citizens, though the implementation details will be crucial in determining its success and mitigating associated risks.

The debate around state police is deeply intertwined with Nigeria's federal structure and the balance of power between the central government and its constituent states. Historically, the federal government has maintained a tight grip on security institutions, a legacy of military rule and a desire to prevent regional strongmen from accumulating too much power. However, the current federal police force is widely perceived as underfunded, understaffed, and overstretched, unable to effectively police a nation of over 200 million people with diverse security challenges. The recent presidential directives for intensified operations against bandits and structural reforms like state police are a direct response to this systemic failure. Communities, often left to fend for themselves or even resorting to paying levies to bandits, are desperate for improved security. The working group's task is not just to draft a bill but to navigate the complex political, financial, and logistical hurdles of such a monumental shift. The outcome will be closely watched, as it could either usher in a new era of localized security effectiveness or introduce new layers of complexity and potential conflict.

### CPPE Urges CBN to Rethink Development Finance, Says Real Sector Faces N50tn Funding Gap (Premium Times)

**What happened:** The Centre for the Promotion of Private Enterprise (CPPE) has called on the federal government and the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) to overhaul the country's development finance framework. In a policy brief signed by CEO Muda Yusuf, the advocacy group warned that Nigeria's productive sectors, including manufacturing, agriculture, agribusiness, and MSMEs, face a colossal financing shortfall exceeding N50 trillion. The CPPE argued that the current financial system cannot provide the affordable, long-term funding these sectors desperately need, citing high lending rates (CBN's MPR at 26.5%, CRR at 45%), short loan tenors, stringent collateral requirements, and limited risk appetite among lenders.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a significant intervention that directly challenges the current direction of the "CBN Policy and Economic Management" storyline, which has seen the CBN curtailing development finance interventions to focus on price and monetary stability. The CPPE's estimate of a "conservative current real-sector financing gap of over N50 trillion" is a staggering figure that underscores the severe structural market failures hindering Nigeria's economic growth. This directly connects to the "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storyline, highlighting the persistent inflation and external liabilities as challenges, but also emphasizing that a lack of productive capacity is a major driver of inflation, not just excess demand. The CPPE argues that "price stability and development finance should not be treated as mutually exclusive objectives," advocating for carefully targeted, transparently governed, and non-inflationary interventions.

The historical context is crucial here: agriculture, despite contributing over one-fifth of Nigeria's GDP, has received less than five percent of total banking sector credit. Manufacturers, needing long-term capital for machinery, technology, and expansion, cannot rely on short-term commercial loans. The CPPE acknowledges past governance shortcomings in CBN intervention programs but insists these "justify reforms rather than abandoning development finance altogether." Their proposed model shifts the CBN's role to a catalyst, refinancer, and risk-sharing institution, with development finance institutions and commercial lenders handling appraisal and recovery. This policy brief provides a detailed roadmap for addressing a fundamental economic bottleneck, suggesting a potential pivot in the CBN's strategy that could significantly impact the real sector and, by extension, job creation and overall national progress. It also implicitly touches on the "Experts Warn Nigeria Against 'New Oil Curse'" storyline by emphasizing the need to add value to raw materials locally, which requires significant investment in productive sectors.
