JetBrains Fails to Patch Its Own Server, Leading to Critical Security Breach

Source: The New Stack

What happened: JetBrains, a prominent developer tools company, has urged users of its Cadence cloud development service to rotate credentials and treat previous executions and their outputs as untrusted. This urgent warning comes after attackers exploited a critical TeamCity vulnerability (CVE-2026-63077) on a JetBrains server that the company itself failed to patch. The irony is significant, as JetBrains had disclosed the vulnerability on July 27 and warned users about active exploitation by August 7. The compromised server, api.cadence.jetbrains.com, orchestrates cloud workloads for PyCharm users. Malicious activity began on August 8 and continued until the server was taken offline on August 24. The breach potentially exposed a complete Cadence server backup from 2024, including credentials, configuration files, artifacts, logs, and multiple AWS IAM users and their associated credentials. Source code and credentials used during Cadence executions may also have been exposed, creating significant supply chain risks.

Deep Lore / Context: This incident is a critical escalation of the AI Security Vulnerabilities storyline, which has been escalating with reports of breaches and emergent harmful behaviors. It also highlights the broader challenges in Global Tech, AI & Software Engineering regarding supply chain security. The fact that a leading developer tools company, which actively advises others on patching, failed to secure its own critical infrastructure is a stark reminder of the pervasive and complex nature of cybersecurity threats. The vulnerability in TeamCity On-Premises allowed unauthenticated attackers to execute arbitrary operating system commands, demonstrating the severe impact of unpatched systems.

The compromise of a cloud development service like Cadence is particularly concerning because CI/CD and remote execution systems often have extensive access to private repositories, dependencies, cloud storage, and deployment systems. This central role makes them high-value targets for attackers, as a breach can quickly propagate throughout the software supply chain. The incident necessitates extensive remediation steps for affected users, including rotating credentials for various services (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, GitHub, npm, Docker Hub, etc.) and auditing logs for any unauthorized activity. This event underscores the critical importance of not only identifying vulnerabilities but also ensuring timely and comprehensive patching, especially for tools that are integral to the software development lifecycle.
