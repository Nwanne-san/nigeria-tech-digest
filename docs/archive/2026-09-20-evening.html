SERAP Sues INEC, Demands Disclosure Of Political Funding Limits * What happened: The Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP) has filed a lawsuit against the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), seeking an order to compel the electoral body to disclose the limits on political contributions under Section 91 of the Electoral Act 2026. SERAP also wants to know if INEC has set such limits and why they haven't been publicly disclosed. The lawsuit further requests information on the systems and procedures in place for monitoring and enforcing compliance with these limits and campaign expenditure, especially in preparation for the 2027 general elections. * Deep Lore / Context: This action by SERAP is a critical move in the ongoing struggle for transparency and accountability in Nigeria's political financing. The Electoral Act 2026, like its predecessors, aims to regulate how political parties and candidates raise and spend money to ensure a level playing field. However, concerns about the "monetisation of elections" and opaque sources of funding have been persistent issues in Nigerian politics. SERAP's argument hinges on the principle that transparency in political financing is essential for citizens to make informed choices and for elections to be genuinely competitive, not predetermined. The timing, with the 2027 elections on the horizon, makes this demand particularly urgent, as it could impact campaign strategies and the fairness of the electoral process. The broader context involves a history of weak regulation and enforcement in political finance in Nigeria. Despite constitutional provisions (Sections 225 and 226) that empower INEC to examine political party finances and report to the National Assembly, there have been persistent allegations of systemic non-compliance by parties and a lack of effective sanctions. SERAP’s lawsuit is an attempt to leverage the judiciary to force INEC to fulfill its oversight responsibilities and to ensure that the rules governing political donations and spending are clear and accessible to the public.
