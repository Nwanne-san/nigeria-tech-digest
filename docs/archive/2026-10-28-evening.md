This necessitates "stateless edge verification" using cryptographic methods like JSON Web Key Sets (JWKS) caching, rather than centralized RPCs, to prevent authorization from becoming a bottleneck. The article also emphasizes the need for "observe mode" in agent security, mapping machine-to-machine credentials and auditing tool usage before strict policy enforcement.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a critical deep dive into the infrastructure realities behind our **AI Model Competition and Enterprise Adoption** storyline, which has been escalating with intense competition and significant investments in efficiency. OpenAI's 1.2 billion WAUs for ChatGPT is a staggering figure, demonstrating the unprecedented scale of AI adoption. The technical challenges of managing Identity and Access Management (IAM) at this "extreme web scale" are profound, moving beyond traditional human-centric authentication to "agentic workloads." This means that instead of just authenticating a user, systems must now authenticate and authorize a multitude of AI agents acting on behalf of that user, often in rapid, automated sequences.

This also directly connects to our **Adversarial AI Evolution and Cybersecurity** storyline. As AI agents become more autonomous and pervasive, securing their interactions and ensuring proper authorization becomes a paramount cybersecurity concern. The concept of "observe mode" for agent security, which involves fingerprinting agents, tracing their execution, and implementing scoped identity delegation, is a crucial development. It reflects a proactive approach to managing the risks associated with increasingly powerful and autonomous AI systems. For developers, this signals a shift towards more sophisticated, distributed, and edge-native authentication and authorization patterns, moving away from monolithic legacy IAM solutions.
