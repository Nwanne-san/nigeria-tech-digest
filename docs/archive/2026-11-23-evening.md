### Africa wants AI. Can its startups afford to build it?

### How Sinch modernized its enterprise data warehouse from Snowflake to BigQuery to power AI
**Source:** Google Cloud Blog

**What happened**
Sinch, one of the world’s leading cloud communications and customer engagement (CPaaS) companies, successfully migrated its enterprise data warehouse from a distributed, multi-cloud Snowflake setup (across AWS and GCP with separate instances in EU, US, and Australia) to Google BigQuery. This modernization journey was driven by the need to eliminate data silos, curb monthly billing spikes, establish a single governed source of truth, and unlock the next generation of predictive AI capabilities. Sinch partnered with Google Cloud Professional Services, leveraging Dataproc Serverless (running Apache Spark 3.5) for optimized data ingestion from S3 and Databricks Unity Catalog into native BigQuery tables. Crucially, they utilized Google Cloud BigQuery Migration Service alongside custom AI translation skills, including the dbt-sf-to-bq-translator, to automate the conversion of over 1,200 dbt models from Snowflake to BigQuery SQL syntax, significantly reducing manual effort and error rates. The migration resulted in substantial cost savings, improved data validation, and strategically positioned Sinch for advanced enterprise AI applications.

### AWS launches 5 new open decision models for AI agents
**Source:** The New Stack

**What happened**
AWS on Monday launched Strands Decider v2, a family of five open decision models. These models, based on Google’s Gemma 4, range in size from 2 billion to a 31-billion-parameter dense model and are available under the Apache 2.0 license. Unlike traditional LLMs that generate text, Strands Decider models take a state (e.g., a support ticket) and a question with a fixed set of possible answers, returning a probability for each. AWS VP Marc Brooker noted that the new version offers significantly better accuracy and calibration than v1, with the 31B model averaging 82.1 across six public text benchmarks, outperforming Google’s Gemini Decision 1 on some. Internal Amazon use cases showed the v1 E4B model cut average call time from 4,215 milliseconds to 897 milliseconds and costs from $22,200 to $500 per million tickets for labeling support tickets.

**Context**: This release is a direct escalation of the "AI Model Competition and Enterprise Adoption" storyline, as tech giants continue to push innovation in foundational AI models and agentic systems. The significant cost and latency reductions demonstrated internally underscore the practical enterprise value of these models, further driving AI adoption.
