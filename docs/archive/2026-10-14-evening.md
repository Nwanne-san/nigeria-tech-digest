This advice comes as the FG recently announced a 30-day petrol discount at NNPC Limited retail stations, prioritizing public transport operators, to mitigate the impact of rising fuel prices. Separately, the IMF also urged the FG to boost revenue and cut waste to address rising debt.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a direct continuation and reinforcement of the "CBN Policy and Economic Management" and "National Progress & Economy" storylines, which have seen ongoing debates around the removal of fuel subsidies and broader fiscal reforms. The argument that subsidies are regressive and costly aligns with the government's initial rationale for removal, but the socio-economic impact has been severe, leading to calls for their restoration, especially ahead of an election year. This creates a delicate balancing act for the government: maintaining fiscal discipline as advised by international bodies versus addressing public outcry and the cost of living crisis.

**Headline:** 66% of billion-dollar companies leave critical bugs unpatched — the part the AI bug-hunting hype leaves out (The New Stack)

Chainguard Harness has already uncovered serious vulnerabilities in codebases, including a now-patched flaw in vLLM, an open-source inference engine, and other critical infrastructure software findings that are currently under coordinated disclosure. The launch is part of a four-product release, extending Chainguard’s security reach into agent execution environments and CI verification. A survey commissioned by Chainguard, "The AI Exploit Gap," revealed that over half of security leaders, and 66% of those at companies with $1 billion or more in revenue, knowingly leave high or critical vulnerabilities unpatched at least sometimes. Quincy Castro, Chainguard’s CISO, noted that "AI can find and chain flaws in minutes, while fixing them still moves at human speed."

**Deep Lore / Context:** This development is a significant advancement in the "Adversarial AI Evolution and Cybersecurity" storyline, demonstrating how AI is being leveraged to combat the very vulnerabilities that adversarial AI might exploit. It also strongly connects to "AI Model Competition and Enterprise Adoption" and "Generative Software and Interface World Models" by showcasing the practical application of frontier models in critical enterprise functions. The statistic that 66% of billion-dollar companies leave critical bugs unpatched is alarming and underscores a fundamental challenge in cybersecurity: the sheer volume and complexity of vulnerabilities outpace human capacity to identify and remediate them. This "AI Exploit Gap" highlights the urgent need for automated, intelligent security solutions.

Chainguard Harness's ability to "prove a vulnerability is exploitable before it counts" by reproducing potential exploits in isolated environments, and then having a human engineer approve every fix, addresses a key concern with AI-generated reports—the potential for noise and false positives. This human-in-the-loop approach is crucial for building trust and ensuring the reliability of AI-driven security. For software development, this heralds a transformative shift, where AI agents can run CI checks, inspect failures, and revise code before pull requests, potentially streamlining the development pipeline significantly. However, as Quincy Castro points out, the bottleneck remains human speed in patching, suggesting that while AI can accelerate vulnerability discovery, the organizational and operational challenges of remediation persist, creating a continuous arms race between AI-powered offense and defense.
