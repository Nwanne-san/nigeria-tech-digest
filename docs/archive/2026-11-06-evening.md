### Nigeria Rewriting Cyber Laws Amid AI-Driven Threats, Acknowledges Governance Gap
**Source:** TechCabal

**What happened:** Nigeria is actively reviewing and rewriting its cybersecurity laws to combat escalating threats, particularly as Artificial Intelligence (AI) makes fraud, digital deception, and attacks on critical infrastructure increasingly difficult to detect. AI was linked to 55% of cybercrime incidents in Africa last year, with deepfake cases increasing sevenfold between Q2 and Q4 2024. The government acknowledges that existing frameworks are inadequate and do not align with international best practices. This legislative overhaul includes amendments to the Cybercrimes (Prohibition, Prevention, etc.) Act 2015, a review of the National Cybersecurity Policy and Strategy (due for its five-year review in 2026), and the proposed National Digital Economy and E-Governance Bill 2025. Adedeji Stanley Olajide, Chairman of the House Committee on ICT & Cybersecurity, expressed confidence that these bills would be enacted between January and June 2027, aiming to curb cyberbullying, enforce stricter compliance in the financial sector, and establish a national cybersecurity architecture.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This development is a direct **escalation** in our "AI Governance and Regulation Gap" storyline, highlighting Nigeria's urgent response to the policy vacuum surrounding rapidly advancing AI. It also significantly impacts "Adversarial AI Evolution and Cybersecurity" and "Security Operations and Insecurity Challenges (Nigeria)". The government's recognition that AI is accelerating cyber threats faster than defenses can be built is a critical admission. The financial sector, in particular, is under siege, having lost ₦25.85 billion ($19.2 million) to electronic payment fraud in 2025, with the CBN cautioning banks and fintechs about AI-driven vulnerabilities. This aligns with findings from TC Insights' "The Compliance Reckoning" report, which noted the growing sophistication of AI-powered attacks despite a decrease in gross fraud losses. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) has also been active, fining MultiChoice Nigeria ₦766.24 million ($568,630) for data breaches and investigating 1,369 organizations.

Beyond the financial sector, government institutions themselves have proven vulnerable. The AI-generated photo controversy involving INEC's voter register, the Corporate Affairs Commission's breach exposing over 15 million corporate documents, and Remita's data exposure due to a misconfigured cloud storage bucket all underscore systemic weaknesses. The NDPC National Commissioner, Vincent Olatunji, revealed his commission faced over 2,000 cyberattack attempts in one week. These incidents illustrate how AI-enabled behavioral evasion allows threat actors to mimic legitimate user actions, rendering traditional security controls obsolete. The legislative reforms aim to establish clear accountability and enforce proper configuration standards, but their true efficacy will depend on improved enforcement, fortification of vulnerable infrastructure, and the development of local technical expertise, rather than merely expanding state surveillance powers. The push for a National Digital Cloud Policy, which includes tax waivers for data center infrastructure and a mandate for sensitive national data to remain within Nigerian borders, indicates a strategic effort towards cybersecurity sovereignty, though capital targets alone won't guarantee enhanced security capacity.

### White House Network Exposed to Residential Proxy Activity, Raising Cybersecurity Concerns
**Source:** The Verge

**What happened:** Security firms Layer3 Intel and Synthient discovered residential proxy activity on the White House network, indicating that software on devices within the presidential network could be enabling outsiders to rent access to its IP addresses for "a few dollars." This activity, typically occurring between 5 PM ET and midnight, suggests a personal device infected with a proxy SDK, possibly linked to the watch party app Rave, which bundles 12 proxy SDKs. **Deep Lore / Context:** While this event is not directly about Nigeria, it offers a crucial global context for the "Adversarial AI Evolution and Cybersecurity" storyline and the broader challenges of digital security that Nigeria is also grappling with. This isn't a sophisticated state-sponsored hack in the traditional sense, but rather a vulnerability introduced by third-party software on a user's device, demonstrating that the weakest link can often be human behavior or poorly vetted applications. The ease with which access can be rented for "a few dollars" underscores the commodification of cyber vulnerabilities.

For Nigeria, which is currently rewriting its cyber laws and struggling with systemic vulnerabilities in its financial and government institutions, this serves as a stark warning. As AI accelerates cyber threats and introduces new attack vectors, the focus cannot solely be on perimeter defenses or large-scale infrastructure. It must also extend to endpoint security, user education, and rigorous vetting of all software, especially those with third-party SDKs. The incident illustrates how "weaponized internal trust" and compromised credentials, as noted in the TechCabal article on Nigeria's cyber threats, can be exploited.
