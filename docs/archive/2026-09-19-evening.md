### State Weaponizes Law Against Dissent Under Tinubu’s Watch
**Source:** Premium Times

**What happened:** An opinion piece by Kolawole Olaniyan in Premium Times highlights a disturbing trend under President Bola Tinubu's administration: the alleged weaponization of the criminal justice system, particularly by the State Security Service (SSS), to silence peaceful dissent and intimidate critics. The article cites recent cases involving the Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP), Professor Pat Utomi, and opposition activist Omoyele Sowore. SERAP, after two decades of human rights advocacy, faces judicial harassment, including a N100 million defamation judgment. Professor Utomi was taken to court by the SSS over his proposal for a "shadow government," while Omoyele Sowore faces a cybercrime lawsuit for allegedly calling President Tinubu a "criminal" on social media.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This report marks a significant escalation in the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline, specifically concerning the shrinking civic space and freedom of expression. Nigeria has a history of state repression, but the article argues that under Tinubu, it's being compounded by the misuse of legal and security apparatus. The SSS, an agency meant to protect the state, is increasingly found in court seeking to restrain citizens from speaking and organizing. This is particularly ironic given that the current National Security Adviser, Nuhu Ribadu, was once defended by SERAP against similar harassment. The cases against SERAP, Utomi, and Sowore suggest a pattern where legitimate political criticism and public interest advocacy are being re-framed as threats to national security or criminal offenses. This approach risks chilling legitimate criticism and public-interest advocacy, undermining democratic principles and the rule of law, despite President Tinubu's public statements about his commitment to tolerance for criticism. The core issue is whether the state's legitimate powers are being used proportionately or as a pretext for suppressing dissent.

The implications are profound for civil society organizations, journalists, and everyday citizens. When expressing critical views of authorities can lead to criminal charges or costly lawsuits, it creates an environment of fear and self-censorship. This contrasts sharply with the expectation in a democracy where public officials should be accountable and open to scrutiny. The article suggests that the current administration's actions are making a "mockery of Nigeria’s constitutional and international obligations to respect human rights and uphold the rule of law." The international condemnation of the judgment against SERAP by organizations like Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch further underscores the global concern over this trend, signaling a potential erosion of democratic norms and a move towards a more authoritarian style of governance.
