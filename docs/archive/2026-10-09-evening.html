Headline: AWS, Upstage and Ollama agree on a decision-model API. OpenAI hasn\u2019t signed on. (The New Stack)

What happened: Within three weeks, decision models have emerged as a new category in AI, with AWS, Upstage, and Ollama agreeing on a standardized API for these models. AWS's new Strands Decider 2B, an open-weight model, answers at /v1/systemone, the same endpoint introduced by startup TypeSafe AI with its Jev model. This API reduces decisions to three question types (choice, score, yes/no) and returns structured answers with probabilities, never generating prose or explanations. The standardization allows for code-level portability between providers. OpenAI, however, remains an outlier, having launched its own Decisions API built on a specialized GPT-6 Luna, currently in limited preview without a published schema or pricing.

Deep Lore / Context: This rapid standardization of the decision-model API, even without formal governance, is a significant development in the escalating AI Model Competition and Enterprise Adoption and Generative Software and Interface World Models storylines. It mirrors the earlier trajectory of OpenAI's Chat Completions API, which became a de facto standard for LLMs due to widespread cloning. Decision models are designed to act as the "if statement of an AI application," handling the many small, fast judgments an AI agent makes between larger tasks, such as routing requests, gating tool calls, or falling back when a model fails. This structured, probability-based output is a crucial advancement for building reliable and efficient AI applications, especially for enterprise adoption.

The emergence of open-weight models like Strands Decider and Perplexity's pplx-decider, alongside hosted services, offers developers flexibility in deployment, allowing them to run models on their own infrastructure for sensitive application state. This also ties into the broader debate around "open weight" versus "open source" AI, as highlighted by Percona CEO Peter Farkas, who argues that open weights alone don't provide the full freedoms of open source. OpenAI's decision to go its own way with a proprietary Decisions API, built on GPT-6 Luna, indicates its continued strategy of controlling its ecosystem, potentially leading to a bifurcated market with two competing contracts. While TypeSafe is "winning the schema war," the "endpoint war remains open," meaning developers will need to navigate these differing standards, impacting interoperability and potentially slowing broader adoption until a clearer industry consensus emerges.
