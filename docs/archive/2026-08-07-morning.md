### NCAA opposes TSC allocation review, cites risks to aviation safety oversight in Nigeria
**Source:** Nairametrics / Premium Times

**What happened**
The Nigeria Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) Director-General, Chris Najomo, has cautioned against any further reduction in the regulator’s statutory funding, warning that such a move could weaken Nigeria’s aviation safety oversight. Speaking at a public hearing by the House of Representatives Committee on Aviation, Najomo opposed a proposed review of the allocation of the 5% Ticket Sales Charge (TSC) and Cargo Sales Charge (CSC). He explained that the TSC accounts for about 85% of the NCAA’s revenue, making it critical to the authority’s operations. The Airline Operators of Nigeria (AON) proposed abolishing the percentage-based TSC and replacing it with a fixed levy, citing the financial burden on domestic carriers.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This issue highlights a critical tension in public sector financing and regulatory effectiveness within Nigeria, particularly in a sector as vital and sensitive as aviation. The NCAA is the primary regulatory body responsible for ensuring aviation safety in Nigeria, a role that demands significant financial independence, robust technical capacity, and adherence to international standards set by organizations like the International Civil Aviation Organisation (ICAO). Any substantial reduction in its funding directly impacts its ability to conduct thorough inspections, enforce regulations, invest in modern technology, and train specialized personnel, potentially compromising the safety of Nigeria's airspace and its international standing. This is a developing situation that could have far-reaching implications for air travel safety, public confidence, and the overall investment climate in Nigeria's aviation sector.

The debate also involves the financial health of domestic airlines, which are grappling with high operating costs, including fluctuating fuel prices, maintenance expenses, and various taxes and charges. Their call for a fixed levy instead of a percentage-based charge reflects a common industry complaint about the cumulative burden of multiple charges, which can significantly impact profitability and competitiveness. However, the NCAA, supported by other aviation experts present at the hearing, including former Directors-General and Nigeria's representatives to ICAO, argues that the regulator's independence and financial capacity must be preserved. They suggest that any additional funding required by other aviation agencies, such as the Nigerian Airspace Management Agency (NAMA), should first be pursued through improved collection of their statutory commercial revenues, greater operational efficiency, and stronger corporate governance, rather than by weakening the safety regulator. This complex interplay of regulatory needs, industry pressures, and government revenue allocation underscores the persistent challenges in balancing economic viability with critical safety standards in Nigeria's infrastructure sectors.
