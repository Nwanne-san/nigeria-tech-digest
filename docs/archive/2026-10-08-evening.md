### INEC Issues Stern Warning Ahead of 2027 Elections, Citing Supreme Court Judgement
**Source:** Daily Trust

**What happened:** The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has declared that political parties failing to field candidates whose names are on the registers submitted to the commission will be barred from participating in the 2027 general elections. This decisive stance follows the Supreme Court's judgement on September 24, 2026, which upheld the constitutionality of Sections 77(5), 77(6), and 77(7) of the Electoral Act. These sections mandate that parties must submit their membership registers to INEC 30 days before their primaries and that all candidates must be drawn from these verified registers.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a significant escalation in INEC's ongoing efforts to enforce electoral integrity, directly building on the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline we've been tracking. The Supreme Court's affirmation of these specific sections provides INEC with a much stronger legal foundation to sanction non-compliant parties, a power it has long sought to wield more effectively. Historically, Nigerian political parties have often been criticized for opaque internal processes, including the manipulation of membership lists, the conduct of sham primaries, and the imposition of candidates. These practices frequently lead to internal party disputes and protracted legal challenges that can undermine the credibility of elections and extend well beyond the official election period.

The implications for the 2027 elections are profound. Political parties will now be under unprecedented pressure to maintain accurate, transparent, and verifiable membership databases. Their internal primary elections, which are the bedrock of democratic candidate selection, will likely face far closer scrutiny from INEC. For a nation where electoral disputes frequently erode public trust and consume significant judicial resources, this move could be a foundational step towards fostering cleaner, more democratic internal party processes. However, the success of this policy hinges critically on INEC's capacity to consistently and impartially monitor all party registers and primaries across the nation, and whether the political class will genuinely adhere to these rules or seek new, more sophisticated workarounds. This measure is a direct response to the persistent challenges of political integrity and aims to strengthen the institutional framework for future elections.
