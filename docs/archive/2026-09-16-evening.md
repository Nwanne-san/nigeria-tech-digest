### Headline: Ex-NBA President Nwokoye conferred with Blue Silk rank (Vanguard)

**What happened:** Chief Ebele Nwokoye, a former President of the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA) from 1985 to 1987, was conferred with the Blue Silk rank on Tuesday in Abakaliki, Ebonyi State. This honour is introduced as an alternative to the Senior Advocate of Nigeria (SAN) title, specifically for non-litigation lawyers. Nwokoye was also made National Patron of the Body of Senior Counsel of Nigeria (BOSCON), the body responsible for the assessment and conferment of this rank. This event marks a significant twist in a legal battle, as both the NBA and the Chief Justice of Nigeria (CJN), Justice Kudirat Kekere-Ekun, had previously opposed the Blue Silk title, deeming it illegal and lacking statutory backing.

**Deep Lore / Context:**
This development is a direct challenge to the established order within Nigeria's legal profession, continuing a storyline of internal professional governance disputes. The Legal Practitioners’ Privileges Committee (LPPC) had disowned the Blue Silk title, asserting that the SAN rank remains the highest professional distinction and warning that using the Blue Silk could violate the Rules of Professional Conduct for Legal Practitioners 2023. This stance underscores the LPPC's role as the traditional gatekeeper of legal excellence and its perceived authority over such matters. The conflict escalated in 2025 when the Mazi Afam Osigwe-led NBA regime allegedly used "illegal methods" and "self-help" to disrupt the maiden investiture ceremony, including mobilizing police to deny entry to the original venue. This mirrors historical power struggles within professional bodies where new entrants or alternative recognition systems challenge the status quo, often leading to protracted legal and administrative battles.

The current conferment on a former NBA President, particularly after a Federal High Court judgment on January 27, 2026, affirmed the Blue Silk rank and prohibited the NBA and LPPC from regulating matters not explicitly in the Legal Practitioners Act 1962, signifies a major victory for the proponents of the Blue Silk. The Executive Secretary of ALDRAP, Dr Tonye Clinton Jaja, highlighted that the NBA failed to appeal this judgment within the prescribed time, making it binding. This saga is not merely about a title; it's about the evolution of legal practice in Nigeria, recognizing the growing importance of non-litigation expertise, and challenging the traditional, litigation-centric pathways to professional eminence. It raises questions about the adaptability of established institutions to changing professional landscapes and the role of the judiciary in resolving such internal disputes. The outcome could set a precedent for other specialized fields within the legal profession seeking distinct recognition.
