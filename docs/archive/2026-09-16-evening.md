### National Assembly Transmits Constitution (Sixth Alteration) Bill To Houses Of Assembly
**Source:** Channels TV

**What happened**
The National Assembly has officially transmitted the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (Sixth Alteration) Bill, 2026, to the Houses of Assembly of the 36 states for their consideration and approval. This move follows months of legislative pushes within both the Senate and House of Representatives aimed at reshaping key aspects of national governance. The transmission is a critical step in the constitutional amendment process, requiring the assent of at least two-thirds of the state assemblies (24 states) before it can be enacted into law.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This development is a direct continuation of the **Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)** storyline we've been tracking, particularly the intensifying legislative efforts ahead of the 2027 election cycle. Constitutional amendments are often highly contentious, reflecting deep-seated debates about federalism, resource control, and the balance of power. The current push suggests a concerted effort by the National Assembly to address long-standing structural issues, or perhaps to consolidate certain policy directions. The "Sixth Alteration" implies a significant package of changes, the specifics of which will now be debated at the state level. Historically, constitutional amendments in Nigeria have been slow and arduous, often becoming entangled in political maneuvering and regional interests. The 30-day deadline given to the state assemblies by the National Assembly signals an urgency, but also raises questions about the depth of deliberation possible within such a timeframe. The outcome of this process will profoundly impact the operational framework of governance, potentially affecting everything from state autonomy to the distribution of national resources, and could shape the political landscape for years to come.

The transmission of this bill is a significant marker in Nigeria's ongoing democratic evolution. The ability to amend the constitution, while crucial for adapting to contemporary challenges, also presents opportunities for political actors to push agendas that may not always align with broader national interests. The requirement for state assembly approval is a safeguard, but also a potential bottleneck, as state legislatures often align with the political leanings of their governors and the dominant party. The public's scrutiny on economic accountability, which has been growing amidst labor disputes and lingering subsidy reform issues (as noted in previous briefings), will undoubtedly extend to how these constitutional changes are perceived to impact the average Nigerian. The success or failure of this bill will be a testament to the current administration's ability to forge consensus and navigate the complex web of inter-governmental relations.

**Source:** Vanguard

**What happened**
Ayo Adio, the African Democratic Congress (ADC) candidate for Lagos State House of Assembly, Eti-Osa Constituency 2, has criticized Nigerian state governors for their deployment of increased revenues following the removal of petrol subsidy. The significant increase in state revenues was expected to translate into improved public services and development projects, alleviating the economic hardship caused by the subsidy removal and rising petrol prices. However, the data presented indicates a concerning trend where increased funds are not proportionally allocated to these vital sectors, and more states are still resorting to borrowing. This raises serious questions about fiscal discipline, transparency, and accountability at the sub-national level.

The debate over the deployment of subsidy savings is central to public trust and the perceived legitimacy of the government's economic reforms.
