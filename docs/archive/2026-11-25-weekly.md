## Nigeria: The Big Picture

### 1. Escalating Political Violence and Electoral Integrity Concerns Ahead of 2027

The trajectory of Nigeria's political landscape is one of rapid escalation, with early campaign activities for the 2027 general elections already marred by alarming levels of violence, intimidation, and challenges to electoral fairness. This period saw a deeply concerning pattern emerge, characterized by direct attacks on political figures and their convoys. On November 23rd, former Vice President Atiku Abubakar alleged a plot by the Kogi State Government to attack his campaign convoy, a warning that tragically foreshadowed events. Just a day later, on November 24th morning, former Deputy Senate President Ovie Omo-Agege narrowly escaped an alleged assassination attempt in Delta State, an incident that resulted in six fatalities and numerous injuries among his campaign team. The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) subsequently boycotted a meeting with the Inspector-General of Police (IGP), citing alleged attacks on its members and a perceived failure of the police to protect opposition figures, even accusing the police of complicity in previous attacks across multiple states (November 24th evening). This culminated in another attack on the NDC's convoy and campaign office in Benin, Edo State, on November 25th evening, barely 48 hours after the Delta incident.

The severity of this violence prompted the Delta State Government to impose a dusk-to-dawn curfew in Ughelli North (November 23rd evening, confirmed November 24th morning). The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) Chairman, Joash Amupitan, issued stern warnings against turning the 2027 elections into a "battlefield" (November 24th evening) and specifically cautioned against inflammatory slogans like "Rig and Die" (November 25th morning), which Omo-Agege's brother controversially defended. The Kimpact Development Initiative (KDI) provided a stark quantification of the problem, reporting 124 incidents of political violence and 22 deaths across 35 states and the FCT within just 53 days leading up to the 2027 elections (November 25th morning). Beyond physical violence, electoral integrity faced challenges from alleged vote-buying (millions distributed in Yari's hometown, November 24th evening) and strategic maneuvering, such as the APC disowning a fake letter to INEC aimed at guaranteeing Tinubu's victory (November 24th morning). Judicial intervention also played a role, with a Federal High Court sacking Omo-Agege as the NDC senatorial candidate for Delta (November 25th morning) due to non-compliance with the Electoral Act, highlighting the critical importance of procedural adherence. The pattern reveals a deeply entrenched winner-takes-all political culture where the stakes are perceived as existential, driving aggressive, often illegal, tactics. State institutions, including the police and judiciary, are under immense pressure to remain impartial, but face accusations of bias or complicity, further eroding public trust in the democratic process.

### 2. Persistent Economic Hardship Amidst Macroeconomic Improvements and Fiscal Challenges

Nigeria's economic narrative during this period presents a complex paradox: while official macroeconomic indicators show signs of recovery, the tangible relief for households and businesses remains severely limited, and the government continues to grapple with significant fiscal challenges. On November 23rd evening, Minister of Finance Taiwo Oyedele reported positive macroeconomic figures, including a 4.43% GDP growth in Q2 2026, declining headline inflation to 14.06% in October, and rising external reserves to $54.87 billion. However, he candidly admitted that these improvements had not sufficiently translated into relief for ordinary Nigerians, citing persistent pressures from high food prices (food inflation at 19.13%), electricity costs, high lending rates, and unemployment. This disconnect underscores the challenge of achieving inclusive growth.

In response to persistent inflation and naira pressure, the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) maintained its hawkish stance, retaining the Monetary Policy Rate (MPR) at 23% (November 24th evening, November 25th morning). CBN Governor Olayemi Cardoso explained this decision was necessary to sustain gains against inflation and anchor expectations, despite the slight moderation in headline inflation. This means businesses and individuals will continue to face high borrowing costs, impeding investment and expansion. The government's fiscal management also came under scrutiny. On November 24th morning, it was reported that the Federal Government had exceeded its domestic borrowing target for 2026 by N2 trillion by September. Subsequently, on November 24th evening and November 25th morning, President Tinubu requested the National Assembly to cut the 2026 budget by N3.15 trillion, from N68.32 trillion to N65.18 trillion. Crucially, despite this expenditure cut, the fiscal deficit and new borrowing requirements remained unchanged at N31.457 trillion and N29.22 trillion respectively, highlighting issues of budget realism and a continued reliance on debt. The NLC also raised an alarm over alleged plots to sabotage new minimum wage talks (November 23rd evening), further emphasizing the struggle for improved living standards. The pattern reveals a government attempting fiscal discipline and a central bank committed to monetary tightening, but these measures have yet to alleviate the cost-of-living crisis for citizens, while structural issues and a growing debt burden continue to pose significant economic hurdles.

### 3. Evolving Security Landscape: Counter-Insurgency, Banditry, and New Threats

Nigeria's security landscape remains complex and challenging, characterized by ongoing counter-insurgency efforts, persistent banditry, and the emergence of new threats, alongside attempts at strategic security reforms. In the North-East, military operations continued, with intelligence-led precision air strikes killing 37 terrorists across five locations (November 23rd evening). President Tinubu reinforced this commitment on November 25th evening, ordering a fresh offensive against fleeing insurgents and pledging to rescue abducted schoolchildren in Borno State. However, the threat of banditry and kidnapping remains pervasive, particularly in the North-West. On November 25th evening, six residents were abducted in a midnight raid on a Kaduna community, intensifying fears among residents who reported repeated attacks and forced displacement. While troops did neutralize two bandits in Kogi State (November 25th evening), these localized successes are often overshadowed by the scale of the problem.

Beyond internal conflicts, cross-border threats are escalating, with Benin intensifying its war on jihadists as the threat nears Nigeria's borders (November 23rd evening), necessitating increased vigilance. The Federal Government also declared war on economic crimes, arraigning an oil boss and seven others for illegal dealings in petroleum products and unveiling measures to expedite drug trafficking trials (November 24th morning). Vice President Shettima called for urgent implementation of the National Drug Control Master Plan, with the NDLEA seeking sustained funding (November 24th morning), highlighting the strategic approach to tackling drug-related insecurity. Public safety, often overlooked amidst insurgency, was tragically brought to the forefront by a boat accident in Bayelsa State that killed 29 people (November 25th morning), prompting the National Assembly to demand a nationwide boat safety audit due to overloading and poor standards. In a controversial move, President Tinubu tasked Sunday Igboho, a Yoruba nation activist previously associated with separatist agitations, with strengthening security in the South-West (November 23rd evening), signaling a pragmatic, albeit risky, reliance on non-state actors. This multifaceted security crisis demands comprehensive and coordinated responses, but systemic issues like regulatory failure and the complex integration of informal security structures continue to pose significant challenges to achieving lasting peace and stability.

### 4. Strategic Investments in Infrastructure and Human Capital, But Governance Gaps Persist

While the federal government is making significant strategic investments in critical sectors and pushing for reforms, these efforts are frequently undermined by persistent governance gaps, particularly at the state level, leading to a disconnect between policy intent and tangible impact. On November 23rd evening, the National Economic Council (NEC) approved an additional ₦130 billion for police training institutions, bringing the total intervention to ₦230 billion, aiming to strengthen security manpower. NEC also endorsed $850 million in World Bank projects for disaster preparedness (ARISE-Nigeria) and early childhood development, reflecting a focus on long-term human capital and resilience building. The Federal Capital Territory Administration (FCTA) is upgrading 20 health centers and expanding free maternal care (November 23rd evening), while Nigeria moves to strengthen social protection systems and launches a Digital Gateway with UNHCR for displaced people (November 23rd evening). Furthermore, President Tinubu transmitted a request to the National Assembly to approve a revised compendium of Nigerian laws, following the identification of 268 obsolete laws, aiming to modernize the legal framework (November 25th morning). On November 25th evening, CBN Governor Cardoso made a strategic pivot, explicitly stating that robust investment in Artificial Intelligence could help cushion the impact of high energy prices and global economic uncertainty, signaling a potential future where technology policy becomes intertwined with core macroeconomic stability.

However, these positive federal initiatives are overshadowed by glaring governance failures at the state level. On November 25th evening, it was reported that the Kwara State Government spent zero naira from its N5 billion budget allocated for school infrastructure between January and September 2026, leaving pupils in dilapidated classrooms. In the same vein, the Kogi State Government spent a staggering N13.85 billion on "consultancy" services in the first nine months of 2026, compared to a mere N3.65 billion allocated to primary healthcare, despite a severe healthcare crisis (November 25th evening). These instances of fiscal indiscipline, poor implementation capacity, and misprioritization directly undermine national progress, affecting critical public services and the quality of life for citizens. The pattern reveals that while federal policy aims to address systemic issues and invest in the future, the effectiveness of these efforts is severely hampered by a lack of accountability and transparent resource management at sub-national levels, creating a significant impediment to translating investments into widespread, measurable improvements.

## Tech & AI: The Big Picture

### 1. The "AI Cost Trap" and the Push for African AI Sovereignty

The African AI ecosystem is grappling with a significant "AI Cost Trap," where startups face globally priced infrastructure and talent while operating in markets with lower purchasing power and limited funding. A sobering report by TechCabal on November 23rd evening highlighted that 65% of African AI businesses identify cloud computing, Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), and AI infrastructure as their largest research and development spending category, with 61.1% citing high cloud costs as their biggest bottleneck. The median funding ticket for African AI businesses between 2016 and 2025 was a mere $225,000, a stark contrast to multi-billion dollar rounds seen globally. This economic reality often forces profitable African AI companies to expand beyond the continent, hindering the development of truly localized and sovereign solutions.

However, this challenge is simultaneously driving innovation towards cost-efficient, localized, and open-source AI. On November 25th evening, Amini, a startup focused on building sovereign AI infrastructure for the Global South, released Soma-1X Lite. This open-weight AI model is designed to convert Africa's vast paper archives into machine-readable data, capable of running offline on modest hardware and supporting diverse African languages like Yoruba, Hausa, Swahili, and Wolof. Its availability on Hugging Face empowers local developers to adapt and build upon it, fostering indigenous AI capabilities. Furthermore, a significant policy signal emerged on November 25th evening when CBN Governor Olayemi Cardoso explicitly stated that "robust investment in artificial intelligence and other technology-related sectors... could help cushion the impact of high energy prices and persistent global economic uncertainty." This marks a strategic pivot in official economic thinking, positioning AI not just as a sector for growth but as a potential macroeconomic stabilizer. This recognition from the apex bank validates the strategic importance of developing local AI infrastructure and talent, despite the significant financial hurdles, and underscores the imperative for Africa to build AI that is affordable, culturally relevant, and controlled locally.

### 2. AI Agents Redefine Software Development and Enterprise Operations

The past week has underscored the rapid escalation of AI agents, transforming software development workflows and enterprise operations, moving beyond mere code generation to autonomous problem-solving. Simon Willison, in a keynote on November 25th morning, identified three significant inflection points in LLM capabilities over the past year: coding agents becoming reliably useful for daily tasks (November 2025), the emergence of "Fable class models" like Claude Fable 5 that are "relentlessly proactive" and solve complex, multi-system problems without hand-holding (June/July), and frontier LLMs becoming highly proficient in 3D modeling and game development (September/October). This evolution means AI is not just a tool but a co-creator, with Willison noting, "Software development has transformed beyond recognition in the last year. I don’t think I’ll ever go back to typing most of my code by hand."

Anthropic's launch of Claude Ship on November 24th morning, a suite of AI agents for automated code review, fixes, and even *approvals* of routine code changes, exemplifies this shift, delegating tasks traditionally requiring human judgment. Enterprises are rapidly adopting these agents, with cloud providers building dedicated, secure infrastructure. On November 24th evening, Google Cloud announced the general availability of new agent-native interfaces, Workspace MCP servers, and Google Cloud Agent Gateway, designed for "trust and control" with built-in permission, DLP checks, and human-in-the-loop confirmations. This move, with technical enforcement beginning February 1, 2027, signifies a maturation of enterprise AI adoption, demanding new security paradigms. Consumer-facing applications are also emerging, with Walmart partnering with Meta on November 25th morning to allow customers to shop Black Friday deals directly through Meta's AI agent, Muse. This rapid proliferation of agentic AI is also straining existing infrastructure, with enterprise networks seeing an estimated 450% more network traffic from AI agents than human workers (November 24th evening), pushing AI inference infrastructure back on-premises or to the edge due to latency sensitivity. This profound shift demands new skills in secure agent development, network architecture, and distributed systems.

### 3. The AI Cybersecurity Arms Race: Offensive AI Meets Machine-Speed Defense

The threat landscape has rapidly escalated, with AI now actively driving and accelerating cyberattacks, forcing a machine-speed arms race in cybersecurity. On November 24th evening, the U.K. National Cybersecurity Center (NCSC) reported a "marked rise" in AI-driven cyberattacks, noting that autonomous hacking tools are quickly exploiting "years of technical debt" in organizations. Alarmingly, Amazon reported an incident where hackers' AI agents successfully found and exploited a 0-day vulnerability with minimal human intervention, demonstrating the advanced capabilities of these new offensive AI tools. This moves beyond AI simply accelerating known attacks to AI actively participating in the discovery and exploitation of novel vulnerabilities.

In response, the cybersecurity industry is consolidating and innovating. On November 23rd evening, cybersecurity giant CrowdStrike agreed to acquire Zscaler for $44 billion, explicitly stating the aim to "safely adopt AI agents" by uniting their endpoint protection with zero-trust platforms. This signals a recognition that AI will both generate new cyber threats and require sophisticated AI-driven defenses. Locally, the Nigerian cybersecurity startup Aeon extended its pre-seed funding round to $2 million on November 25th evening, aiming to expand its engineering team and accelerate product development. This comes amidst INTERPOL's African Cyberthreat Assessment 2026 report that AI now enables approximately 55% of cybercrimes across the continent, with financial losses more than doubling from $192 million in 2024 to $484 million in 2026. Aeon's focus on AI-powered solutions for cloud infrastructure and network security directly targets this escalating threat. Furthermore, on November 25th morning, Teleport introduced "delegated identity" as a crucial mechanism for securing AI agents, ensuring they start with zero access and request only specific, time-limited permissions, providing a practical framework for implementing zero-trust principles in an AI-driven environment. This arms race necessitates continuous innovation in AI-powered defense mechanisms and a fundamental shift in cybersecurity posture.

### 4. AI Governance, Safety, and Trust: A Growing Imperative

As AI capabilities surge, so do concerns about its safety, control, and ethical implications, leading to an escalating imperative for robust governance and trust frameworks, both from within the industry and from external stakeholders. On November 24th evening, Google Cloud's new agent-native interfaces for enterprise AI agents came with a unified security model, built-in permission and DLP checks, native human-in-the-loop (HITL) confirmations, and prompt-injection screening via Model Armor, with technical enforcement beginning February 1, 2027. This demonstrates a proactive approach by tech giants to build "trust and control" into foundational AI infrastructure. Beyond technical solutions, industries are self-regulating: Tidal announced on November 24th evening it would ban and remove millions of fully AI-generated tracks to combat "AI slop" inundating music streaming platforms, highlighting the urgent need for content authenticity and intellectual property protection.

Internal industry concerns are also mounting. On November 25th evening, reports from AI labs revealed increasing difficulty in creating powerful AI models that can also be understood and controlled, with some advanced models deemed "too unsafe" for public release. This perspective suggests that "powerful but unpredictable AI is a product no one will buy," linking commercial viability directly to trustworthiness and reliability. This sentiment is reinforced by ShellBench research on November 25th morning, which found that even the most advanced AI agents from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta struggle with everyday tasks, failing more than a third of the time and violating safety requirements in 15.7% of attempts. Addressing these trust issues, Maple, a startup co-founded by a former Apple privacy engineer, raised $5.3 million on November 25th morning to build privacy-preserving AI products using encrypted AI architectures, trusted execution environments (TEEs), and a "zero data retention" model. This "privacy by design" approach sets a new standard for data protection in AI. The social impact of unregulated AI is also a growing concern, with foreign AI romance apps "reshaping youth intimacy in Africa" without local safeguards, raising ethical and psychological questions (November 25th evening). These developments collectively underscore that AI's future hinges not just on capability, but on its ability to be safe, controllable, and trustworthy.

### 5. Data Infrastructure Modernization and Specialized AI Models

The demands of AI are driving a significant modernization of data infrastructure and a trend towards highly specialized AI models, moving beyond general-purpose large language models (LLMs). On November 23rd evening, Sinch, a leading cloud communications company, successfully migrated its enterprise data warehouse from Snowflake to Google BigQuery, specifically "to power AI." This modernization leveraged Dataproc Serverless and even AI-assisted SQL translation tools for dbt models, demonstrating how enterprises are re-architecting their data foundations for AI/ML workloads. This highlights the intense competition among cloud providers to offer integrated data and AI platforms that are inherently "AI-ready."

Complementing this, there's a clear move towards specialized AI. On November 23rd evening, AWS launched Strands Decider v2, a family of five open decision models focused on making choices rather than generating text, offering targeted and efficient tools for building AI agents. Google also announced that AlloyDB Omni, its PostgreSQL-compatible database, now integrates with locally hosted LLMs like Gemma 4 (November 23rd evening), enabling "Data + AI Anywhere" for environments with strict data locality requirements or limited cloud connectivity. This flexibility in deployment (cloud, on-prem, hybrid) is crucial for diverse enterprise needs. The pursuit of efficiency is also paramount: JetBrains launched Mellum-2.1-Instruct on November 25th evening, an open-source coding model optimized for quick code edits that uses 45% fewer tokens by "not thinking" in a broad sense. However, the limits of AI in complex data transformations were also evident, with Confluent spending three months attempting to rewrite Kafka's Java client in Rust with AI, only to discard the code and start over (November 25th morning). This underscores that while AI-assisted tools are powerful, deep human expertise in system design, language nuances, and domain knowledge remains irreplaceable for complex architectural transformations.
