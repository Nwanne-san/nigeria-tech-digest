### Tinubu Urges Diaspora Doctors to Return Home, Rebuild Nigeria’s Health Sector
**Source:** BusinessDay, Arise TV

**What happened:** President Bola Tinubu, speaking at the 2026 National Diaspora Day celebration in Abuja, unveiled plans to institutionalize a permanent national framework for engaging Nigerian medical professionals abroad. He called on doctors, nurses, researchers, scientists, and public health experts in the diaspora to play a more active role in rebuilding Nigeria's healthcare system. The President assured them that the Federal Government would create institutional mechanisms to harness their expertise, investments, and innovation, acknowledging their global achievements while maintaining strong ties to their homeland. The National Assembly also pledged legislative backing for the Diaspora Health Impact Initiative (DHII) and renewed its commitment to diaspora voting.

**Deep Lore / Context:**
This appeal by President Tinubu is a direct response to Nigeria's severe "Nigerian Health Sector Crisis" (an ongoing storyline). For years, the country has grappled with a debilitating brain drain, as medical professionals, frustrated by poor working conditions, inadequate infrastructure, low remuneration, and insecurity, have migrated en masse to more developed nations. This exodus has left Nigeria with a critical shortage of skilled healthcare personnel, further straining an already fragile system and making access to quality healthcare a major challenge for ordinary citizens. Previous administrations have made similar appeals, but without a robust, institutionalized framework, these calls have largely failed to reverse the trend.

The significance of this move lies in the explicit commitment to institutionalizing a *permanent* national framework. This suggests a more structured and sustained approach than previous ad-hoc appeals. By involving the National Assembly, the initiative gains legislative weight, potentially leading to policies that provide incentives for returning professionals, facilitate knowledge transfer partnerships with Nigerian universities and hospitals, and encourage investments in local healthcare infrastructure and pharmaceutical manufacturing. This effort is crucial for not only addressing the immediate personnel shortages but also for leveraging the advanced skills and global best practices that diaspora professionals can bring, transforming the brain drain into a "brain gain" or "brain circulation" as noted by the National President of the Nigerian Medical Association, Afekhide Omoti. It's a recognition that Nigeria's human capital abroad is a strategic asset that must be actively engaged for national development.

### Stop Correcting AI Code. Build the System Agents Need.
**Source:** The New Stack

**What happened:** Patrick Debois, coiner of the term DevOps and a technical staff member at Tessl, argued at PlatformCon London that software engineers should shift their focus from correcting AI-generated code to improving the underlying systems that AI agents operate within. He proposed that the "software development lifecycle becomes the context development lifecycle," emphasizing that if an AI agent doesn't perform as desired, the solution lies in enhancing the system and its context, not just tweaking prompts or fixing code. This paradigm shift involves moving from deterministic to non-deterministic and probabilistic systems, requiring engineers to build robust "harnesses" for AI agents.

**Deep Lore / Context:**
This article signals a fundamental evolution in the "AI Agents in Enterprise Observability" storyline and the broader role of software engineering in the age of AI. For years, software development has been characterized by deterministic logic and explicit coding. However, the rise of AI agents that can generate code and perform complex tasks introduces a new, probabilistic dimension. Initially, engineers might have approached AI as a co-pilot, editing its output.
