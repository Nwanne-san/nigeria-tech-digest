**Deep Lore / Context:** This marks a progressive step in Nigeria's legal and social landscape, directly addressing a long-standing human rights concern and aligning Nigeria with global best practices in mental health. This reform is a direct response to persistent advocacy efforts that have highlighted the counterproductive nature of criminalizing mental health crises.

The decision reflects a growing awareness within government circles of the importance of mental health and the need for a more compassionate approach. It connects to broader discussions about human rights and social justice within Nigeria, which have seen various advocacy groups push for reforms across different sectors. While the immediate impact is on individuals attempting suicide, the broader implication is a potential shift in how mental health is perceived and addressed at a policy level. This could pave the way for increased investment in mental health infrastructure, public awareness campaigns, and accessible support services, which are currently severely lacking across the country. The decriminalization is a crucial first step, but its effectiveness will ultimately depend on the availability and accessibility of the promised treatment, care, and support systems. This also implicitly touches on the broader "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storyline by addressing a social welfare issue that impacts productivity and public health.
