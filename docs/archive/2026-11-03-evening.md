### Binance Says Hackers Used Fake Job Interviews and an Internal AI Tool to Pull Off the $6.3 Billion Heist
**Source:** The Verge

**What happened:** Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has revealed details of the largest crypto theft to date, a staggering $6.3 billion heist. According to Binance's postmortem, the attack, attributed by the FBI to a North Korean hacking unit dubbed "TraderTraitor," was a multi-month operation combining well-known hacking techniques with the exploitation of an internal AI tool. The process began in June with a developer downloading a tampered installer from a malicious Google ad, exposing engineer details. In September, another engineer was targeted with fake job interviews, leading them to download malware disguised as an assignment. By October 20th, the attackers used Binance's own internal AI tool to gain control over wallets of the compromised engineers, eventually logging into a third employee's computer on October 29th to launch the attack. Binance stated that its security teams detected the initial malware in June but the sophisticated 0-day malware in the second attack, which replaced a legitimate development tool component, went undetected by endpoint security. The company has replenished 98% of impacted tokens, with about $2.85 billion in stolen assets frozen or intercepted, ensuring no user bears the loss.
