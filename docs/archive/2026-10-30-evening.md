### INEC ICT Director Reveals How AI-Generated Photos Entered Voter Register
**Source:** The Sun, Daily Trust

**What happened**
The Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has attributed the appearance of suspected artificial intelligence-generated photographs and other questionable entries in its preliminary voter register to irregularities allegedly introduced by some registration officers. Dr. Lawrence Bayode, INEC's Director of Information and Communication Technology, disclosed on Arise Television that these officers allegedly used their login credentials to introduce irregular records into the voter database. The commission uncovered these questionable entries through its Automated Biometric Identification System (ABIS), which was deployed to examine the registration records. Bayode explained that while some suspicious photographs were AI-generated, others resulted from "very bad registration procedures." This disclosure follows public concerns raised after INEC uploaded its preliminary register on October 26, with screenshots circulating on social media showing AI-generated photos linked to voter records, particularly in Edo State.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This development marks a significant and concerning escalation in the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline, which has been tracking persistent challenges to electoral integrity, including candidate clearance decisions and corruption cases. The appearance of AI-generated photos in the voter register introduces a new, technologically sophisticated dimension to electoral malpractice, directly undermining the credibility of the foundational document for free and fair elections. Historically, Nigeria's voter registers have been plagued by issues such as ghost voters, underage registrants, and duplicate entries, leading to widespread distrust. INEC's investment in systems like ABIS was precisely to combat these irregularities and enhance transparency. The revelation that internal registration officers, using legitimate login credentials, are allegedly responsible for these breaches points to a deep-seated problem of corruption or sabotage within the electoral machinery itself, rather than external hacking. Bayode's inability to explain "why" these officers acted this way underscores the complexity of the challenge.

The implications of this breach are profound. A compromised voter register can facilitate electoral fraud, disenfranchise legitimate voters, and ultimately distort the democratic will. With the 2027 general elections on the horizon, this incident fuels public skepticism and could lead to increased calls for more robust oversight and accountability within INEC. The fact that the ABIS system *detected* these irregularities is a positive sign of its technical capability, but the vulnerability at the point of data entry by human operators remains a critical flaw. This incident also highlights the double-edged sword of technology in governance: while AI can be a tool for efficiency and verification, it can also be weaponized for manipulation. The ongoing cleanup of the register, with INEC receiving over 4,800 claims and 600 objections, is a necessary step, but restoring public confidence will require more than just technical fixes; it demands a fundamental addressing of human integrity within the system.

**Source:** Nairametrics

The President's call for expeditious Senate approval underscores the urgency placed on ensuring institutional continuity and strengthening the Authority's oversight capacity. Given the ongoing economic reforms and the government's push for increased domestic refining capacity (as seen with the Dangote Refinery IPO), a well-functioning and stable NMDPRA is paramount.
