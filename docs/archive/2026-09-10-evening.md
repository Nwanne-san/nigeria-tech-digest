### Shopify Dumps React Native, Rebuilds Native Apps with AI Agents in 12 Weeks
**Source:** The New Stack, Hacker News

**What happened:** Shopify, a company that famously bet on React Native in 2020 for cross-platform mobile development, has announced it is moving back to full native development for its mobile apps. What's remarkable is the speed of this transition: its consumer app, Shop, was rebuilt natively in just 12 weeks, moving from proof of concept to a fully native production release. This rapid shift was made possible by heavily leveraging AI coding agents. According to Mustafa Ali, Shopify's director of engineering, "LLMs changed one of the core assumptions behind our 2020 decision," as agents became proficient enough by late 2025 to build Android versions from iOS features (and vice-versa), allowing engineers to work across platforms they weren't expert in. Shopify's internal system, Helix, breaks migrations into smaller chunks, comparing results with the existing app and using separate agents for code review before human sign-off.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a monumental story that directly impacts the "Generative Software and Interface World Models" and "AI Agents in Enterprise Observability" storylines, signaling a paradigm shift in software development workflows. Shopify's initial move to React Native was a widely celebrated decision, reflecting the industry's push for efficiency through cross-platform development. Their reversal, driven by the maturity of AI coding agents, demonstrates how quickly AI is reshaping fundamental engineering decisions. The ability of AI agents to generate high-quality, platform-specific code at scale fundamentally alters the cost-benefit analysis of native vs. cross-platform development. This is a powerful validation of the rapid, albeit nascent, development of agent-building capabilities we've been tracking.

The efficiency gains are staggering: Jarred Sumner, Bun creator, used 64 parallel Claude Fable 5 instances to port a million lines of JavaScript runtime code from Zig to Rust in 11 days for an estimated $165,000. This kind of productivity was unimaginable a year ago. However, the article also notes that AI agents can create more work elsewhere in an engineering organization, emphasizing the need for robust review processes and infrastructure adaptations. Shopify's Helix system, with its cautious, human-in-the-loop approach, reflects lessons learned from previous incidents where agent-generated code was shipped without enough review. This move by Shopify is not just about technology; it's about a fundamental re-evaluation of engineering strategy, where codebases are increasingly being designed with AI agents in mind, separating business logic from UI to enable faster, AI-driven testing and development.
