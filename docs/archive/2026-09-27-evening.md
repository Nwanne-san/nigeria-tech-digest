### Falana Sues Nigeria Over Decades of Rights Violations
**Source:** Vanguard
**What happened:** Human rights lawyer and Senior Advocate of Nigeria, Femi Falana, has initiated proceedings before the ECOWAS Community Court of Justice against the Federal Republic of Nigeria. The suit, ECW/CCJ/APP/30/2026, alleges violations of his fundamental rights, including personal liberty, dignity, fair hearing, property, and freedom of movement, spanning several military administrations from the 1980s to the 1990s. Falana recounts incidents such as the withholding of his NYSC certificate, a seven-week detention by the National Security Organisation (NSO) in 1985, an alleged abduction in 1990, and multiple arrests and detentions related to his activism, including challenging the annulment of the June 12, 1993 presidential election. He is seeking $10 million in damages for these alleged violations.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a significant development that touches on Nigeria's long and often turbulent history of human rights abuses, particularly during military rule. Falana's decision to bring this case before the ECOWAS Court, an international judicial body, underscores the persistent struggle for accountability and justice in Nigeria. The case serves as a powerful reminder of the importance of political integrity and electoral processes, a storyline we have been tracking, especially as the 2027 elections approach. It also implicitly questions the current government's commitment to addressing past wrongs, given President Tinubu's attendance at Babangida's memoir presentation without a corresponding apology or remedy for those affected by the annulment.

### "As a Language Model": Chat Template Switches LLM Self-Referential Voice
**Source:** Hacker News (via arXiv)
**What happened:** A new research paper titled "\"As a Language Model...\": Chat Template Switches LLM Self-Referential Voice and Activation Steering Reproduces It" explores the phenomenon of Large Language Models (LLMs) adding disclaimers like "I'm just an AI." The study reveals that the "chat template" used during deployment acts like a switch: when present, it increases the "disclaimer voice" and decreases "experiential voice" (e.g., "I feel"). Conversely, without the template, the disclaimer voice goes down, and experiential voice goes up. Researchers found a specific direction within the activation space of three models that steers this behavior, demonstrating that a model's self-description is not solely from its weights but is partially set by the chat template. This suggests that what models say about themselves shouldn't be treated literally.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This technical insight is highly significant for our **Generative Software and Interface World Models** and **AI Model Competition and Enterprise Adoption** storylines. It provides a deeper understanding of LLM behavior, moving beyond surface-level output to the underlying mechanisms. The finding that a simple "chat template" can significantly alter an LLM's self-referential voice has profound implications for **AI Governance and Regulation Gap** and **AI Existential Risk and Regulation Debate**. Debates about AI safety and self-knowledge often rely on these self-reports, but this research suggests they might be a "confound" rather than a true reflection of the model's internal state or "self-awareness."

For you, an AI engineer, this highlights the critical importance of understanding the deployment context and architectural choices in shaping AI behavior. It also points to the power of "activation steering" as a technique for fine-grained control over model outputs, which could be a powerful tool for customization and safety.
