Source: Vanguard, Punch

What happened: Tragedy struck Kano State with two separate building collapses on Saturday, resulting in five fatalities and ten injuries. The first incident occurred at Gaida in Kumbotso Local Government Area, where an incomplete building under construction, measuring approximately 50 by 50 feet, collapsed, trapping six workers. Firefighters rescued two workers alive (17-year-old Muhammad Hassan and 19-year-old Khalid Abubakar), while four others were recovered unconscious and later confirmed dead (18-year-old Sunusi Surajo, 20-year-old Bashir Ibrahim, 25-year-old Ibrahim Ibrahim, and 30-year-old Abubakar). All six victims were taken to Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital. The second collapse happened at Kurna in Fagge Local Government Area, where the Khadija Idris Bala Islamiyya school collapsed, trapping nine students. Eight students were rescued alive, but 14-year-old Khadija Muhammad was rescued unconscious and later died. The Kano State Fire Service, which responded to distress calls at 2:34 p.m. and 2:40 p.m. respectively, stated that the causes of both collapses are under investigation and urged adherence to safety precautions and building standards, especially during the rainy season.

What happened: Nigeria's foreign exchange derivatives market experienced a dramatic shift, with FX Forwards turnover surging by 263.56% to $90.89 million in the week ended August 14, 2026. This significant increase in forward contracts occurred concurrently with a plunge in FX Spot market activity. The surge in FX Forwards indicates a growing preference among market participants for hedging against future currency fluctuations, suggesting increased uncertainty or anticipation of further Naira volatility despite recent strengthening. The data reflects a strategic move by businesses and investors to lock in exchange rates for future transactions, thereby mitigating risks associated with the unpredictable nature of the Naira.

Deep Lore / Context: This development is a critical update to the CBN Policy and Economic Management storyline, which has been escalating due to the Central Bank of Nigeria's efforts to control inflation and stabilize the Naira. While the Naira has shown some signs of strengthening recently, this surge in FX Forwards, coupled with a plunge in FX Spot, suggests that market confidence in the Naira's immediate stability remains fragile. The FX Forwards market allows participants to buy or sell currency at a predetermined rate on a future date, providing a mechanism to manage exchange rate risk. A significant increase in its activity implies that businesses are actively seeking to de-risk their future foreign currency exposures, indicating a lack of conviction in the current spot market's ability to maintain stability or predict future rates.

This trend could be a response to the CBN's recent benchmark rate hikes and stricter KYC requirements, which aim to combat money laundering and stabilize the currency. However, the market's reaction suggests that while these policies might have had some impact on the spot rate, they haven't fully instilled long-term confidence. The shift towards forwards could also indicate that businesses are anticipating further policy changes or external economic pressures that could impact the Naira. For the broader National Progress & Economy (Nigeria), this signals that despite efforts to stabilize the currency, underlying economic uncertainties persist, prompting market participants to seek more secure, albeit potentially more costly, hedging mechanisms. This could impact import costs, investment decisions, and overall economic planning, as businesses prioritize predictability over immediate spot market access.
