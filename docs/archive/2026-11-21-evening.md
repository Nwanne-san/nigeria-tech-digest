**Source:** Daily Trust

In Anka LGA, Muhammad Ibrahim, a resident, reported that out of 70 villages in Bagega ward, only four remain occupied, with the rest completely deserted. The Anka-Bukkuyyum-Gumi road, once busy, is now empty due to frequent bandit attacks on motorists.

### Naira Depreciates to N1,400/$ Despite CBN's $1.30 Billion Intervention
**Source:** Nairametrics

**What happened**
The Nigerian Naira depreciated by 2.50% against the United States dollar on Friday, November 20, 2026, touching N1,400/$ at the interbank market, despite a significant intervention by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) which sold $1.30 billion to meet surging foreign exchange demand. The local currency's closing rate weakened from N1,360/$ on Monday, November 16, to N1,394/$ on Friday, representing a N34 depreciation per dollar over five consecutive trading sessions. Interbank turnover surged by approximately 1,088% from $68.66 million on Monday to $815.57 million on Friday, totaling about $2.55 billion across 732 transactions for the week. Analysts from Cordros Securities and Cowry Asset Management expect FX demand pressures to persist through the remainder of the year, driven by weaker net foreign inflows, tight global monetary conditions, heightened investor caution ahead of Nigeria's 2027 general elections, and seasonal import demand for the festive period. The naira also depreciated by 2.33% week-on-week in the parallel market to N1,458/$, and weakened across all tenors in the forwards market. Nigeria's gross external reserves declined by $390.94 million over 16 calendar days to $54.87 billion as of November 12. The CBN's Monetary Policy Committee is scheduled to meet on November 23 and 24.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This report directly **escalates** the "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" and "CBN Policy and Economic Management" storylines, both of which have been tracking conflicting narratives on economic recovery, rising business costs, and FX volatility. The naira's continued depreciation, despite a substantial CBN intervention, underscores the deep-seated structural issues and persistent demand-supply imbalance in Nigeria's foreign exchange market. The surge in interbank turnover, while indicating increased activity, does not necessarily translate to sufficient dollar supply to meet demand, leading to further weakening of the currency. This situation is a critical concern for businesses, as it drives up import costs, fuels inflation, and creates uncertainty, impacting investment decisions and overall economic stability.

The analysts' outlook, pointing to sustained FX pressure due to factors like weak foreign inflows, global monetary conditions, and pre-election investor caution, paints a challenging picture for the coming months. The mention of seasonal import demand ahead of the festive period further intensifies demand-side pressures, a predictable but difficult-to-manage economic cycle. The decline in external reserves, though Cordros Securities believes they remain sufficient for intervention, indicates the cost of defending the naira. The upcoming Monetary Policy Committee meeting will be closely watched for further policy responses, as the CBN grapples with the delicate balance of managing inflation, interest rates, and exchange rate stability.
