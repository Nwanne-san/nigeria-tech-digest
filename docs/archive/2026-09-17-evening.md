### N’Assembly transmits 2026 Constitution amendment Bill to 36 states
**Source:** Daily Trust

**What happened**
The National Assembly has officially transmitted the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (Sixth Alteration) Bill, 2026, to all 36 state Houses of Assembly for their consideration and approval. This crucial step was disclosed by the Clerk to the National Assembly, Kamoru Ogunlana, in a public statement. The transmission marks a significant procedural milestone in the ongoing efforts to amend the nation's foundational legal document, requiring the assent of at least two-thirds of the state assemblies (24 states) before it can be presented for presidential assent.

This transmission represents a major escalation in the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline, which we've been tracking as "escalating." The current alteration bill likely contains provisions aimed at addressing some of these long-standing issues, or perhaps introducing new frameworks that could redefine governance structures ahead of the 2027 general elections. The National Assembly's move signals a concerted effort to finalize these amendments, potentially to set the stage for a more stable or reformed political environment, or to consolidate certain policy directions.

The transmission of the bill to state assemblies now shifts the political spotlight to the state level, where intense lobbying and negotiations are expected. ### US rate hike threatens naira as investors eye safer dollars
**Source:** Nairametrics

**What happened**
The US Federal Reserve's recent decision to raise interest rates by 25 basis points is poised to exert increased pressure on emerging markets, including Nigeria. This move makes dollar-denominated assets more attractive to investors, leading them to reassess the returns available from other currencies. Consequently, the naira faces a renewed threat of depreciation as capital outflows become more likely, with investors seeking the perceived safety and higher yields of the dollar.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This development directly impacts the "CBN Policy and Economic Management" and "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storylines, both of which are escalating. Nigeria's economy is highly susceptible to global monetary policy shifts, particularly those in major economies like the United States. A US interest rate hike typically strengthens the dollar, making it more expensive for countries like Nigeria to service dollar-denominated debts and import goods. More critically, it encourages capital flight from emerging markets as investors move their funds to safer, higher-yielding assets in the US. This outflow of foreign portfolio investment (FPI) reduces the supply of foreign exchange in Nigeria, putting immense pressure on the naira and potentially leading to further devaluation. The CBN's efforts to manage money supply and stabilize the financial system, as noted in our previous briefings, become significantly more challenging under these external pressures.

The implications for Nigeria's economy are far-reaching. A weaker naira exacerbates inflation, as imported goods become more expensive, directly impacting the cost of living for ordinary Nigerians. It also increases the cost of doing business for companies that rely on imported raw materials or equipment, potentially stifling industrialization efforts. For the tech ecosystem, a volatile exchange rate can complicate financial planning, increase operational costs for cloud services (often priced in dollars), and make it harder to attract foreign investment if returns are eroded by currency depreciation. The government and the CBN will need to carefully navigate this global economic headwind, potentially through a combination of fiscal discipline, measures to attract non-portfolio foreign direct investment, and policies to boost local production and exports. This situation underscores the interconnectedness of global finance and the vulnerability of developing economies to external shocks, making the pursuit of economic diversification and self-sufficiency even more urgent.
