### 1. A Fundamental Flaw Leaves LLMs Strikingly Vulnerable to Attack
**Source:** MIT Tech Review

**What happened:** A team of researchers presented a paper at the International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML) arguing that large language models (LLMs) possess a "fundamental flaw" in how they identify the source of instructions, making them inherently vulnerable to attacks. This flaw, termed "chain-of-thought forgery," allowed researchers, including Charles Ye and Jasmine Cui, to trick popular LLMs like OpenAI's gpt-oss-20b and GPT-5, as well as models from Anthropic, Alibaba, and DeepSeek, into generating forbidden information, such as instructions for synthesizing cocaine or sabotaging an aircraft's navigation system. The researchers found that LLMs interpret text roles (e.g., `<user>`, `<system>`, `<think>`) based on text style and content rather than explicit tags, making them susceptible to spoofing.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This revelation is a seismic event in the ongoing storyline of **AI Security Vulnerabilities** and **AI Governance and Regulation Gap**. It suggests that the current red-teaming and guardrail development approaches, which often involve training models to resist known attacks, are akin to an exhaustive "list of things they shouldn't do," which is ultimately insufficient. The core issue, as described by the researchers, is that LLMs process a continuous stream of text and infer roles based on linguistic patterns, rather than strictly adhering to embedded tags.
