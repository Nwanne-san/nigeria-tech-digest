### Atiku Flags BVAS Security Risks, Demands INEC Upgrade Ahead of 2027
**Source:** BusinessDay

**What happened**
Atiku Abubakar, the presidential candidate of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), has raised significant concerns regarding the security of the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) ahead of the 2027 general elections. His apprehension stems from reported failures during the August 1 mock accreditation exercise in Osun State and the revelation by Lawrence Bayode, INEC’s Director of ICT, that BVAS currently operates on Android version 10. Atiku highlighted that Android 10 reached its end-of-life status in 2023, meaning it no longer receives crucial security updates or patches. He warned that this outdated operating system creates a "broad attack surface" for hackers to potentially alter cached voter accreditation logs or polling-unit result files before transmission, and could compromise biometric functions. He called for an independent and comprehensive audit of the BVAS hardware, software, source code, and security architecture.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This development marks a significant escalation in the ongoing storyline of "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)," which has been tracking persistent concerns over political integrity, allegations of scams, and intensifying calls for electoral reform ahead of the 2027 elections. The reliability and security of electoral technology, particularly BVAS, have been central to post-election disputes and public trust. The 2023 general elections saw widespread criticism regarding the BVAS's performance and the transparency of result transmission, leading to numerous legal challenges. Atiku's current warning, coming from a prominent political figure, directly taps into these historical anxieties and the public's demand for a credible electoral process. The fact that INEC's ICT Director confirmed the outdated Android version underscores a systemic vulnerability that could undermine the integrity of future polls, making the call for an independent audit a critical step towards rebuilding trust and ensuring a fair democratic exercise.

The issue extends beyond mere software updates; it highlights a broader challenge in Nigeria's digital infrastructure and governance. Running critical national infrastructure like BVAS on an unsupported operating system is a major cybersecurity risk, a point Atiku explicitly made. This situation connects to the larger narrative of national progress and the economy, where investment in robust, secure digital infrastructure often lags behind the pace of technological advancement and the demands of a modern state. ### Databricks Acquires Electric to Give Every AI Agent Its Own Postgres Database
**Source:** The New Stack

**What happened**
Databricks announced its acquisition of Electric, the startup behind the WebAssembly (WASM)-based Postgres project PGlite and the Electric sync engine. The Electric team will join Neon, a serverless Postgres company Databricks acquired last year. PGlite is a complete Postgres database that runs in the browser, Node.js, or agent sandboxes, supporting dynamic extension loading like pgvector. Its weekly downloads surged from 1 million to 13 million over the past year. Databricks' interest lies primarily in Electric's sync engine, which keeps a central Postgres database in near real-time sync with browser tabs, mobile apps, or agents, enabling a "multiplayer model" for agentic applications. Databricks noted that agents now create roughly four times more databases than human users on its Lakebase service, with the average database compute alive for under 10 seconds, reflecting agents' tendency to "branch databases the way they branch code."

**Deep Lore / Context**
This acquisition is a pivotal development for the "AI Agents in Enterprise Observability" storyline, which has been tracking how "the proliferation of AI agents drives investment, but challenges remain in managing scattered AI skills, ensuring secure architectures, and effectively evaluating their output." Databricks' move directly addresses the architectural challenges of agentic applications, particularly the need for ephemeral, highly scalable, and securely managed data storage. The rise of AI agents fundamentally changes how databases are used; instead of a single, permanent database shared by many clients, agents require numerous, short-lived database instances for their specific tasks. This "branching" pattern, akin to code branching, necessitates a new database paradigm that traditional systems struggle to support efficiently.

Electric's PGlite, a WASM-based Postgres, and its sync engine provide a solution by enabling lightweight, client-side databases that can be spun up, used, and discarded rapidly, or synced with a central source. This "database that lives for 10 seconds" concept is revolutionary for agentic workflows, allowing for rapid experimentation, testing, and isolated execution without the overhead of traditional database servers. The acquisition, following Databricks' earlier purchase of Neon, consolidates its position in providing a robust, serverless Postgres foundation for the agentic future. It underscores the growing recognition that the underlying infrastructure for AI agents needs to be reimagined, moving towards more distributed, ephemeral, and agile data management solutions. This trend will significantly impact how developers build, deploy, and manage agentic applications, emphasizing the importance of technologies like WASM and serverless architectures in the evolving AI landscape.
