### **1. Nigeria\u2019s N3.74trn data market faces infrastructure test as network congestion worsens** (BusinessDay)
Nigeria's mobile data market is generating substantial revenue for telecom operators, with MTN Nigeria and Airtel Nigeria earning a combined N3.74 trillion in data revenue between January and September 2026. This translates to approximately N13.7 billion daily, underscoring the immense commercial value of mobile internet usage in the country. However, this rapid expansion of data consumption is placing immense pressure on network infrastructure, leading to widespread subscriber complaints about slow internet speeds, dropped calls, and unreliable connectivity. In response, the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) is considering extending spectrum license tenures from 10 to 15 years to incentivize operators to invest more in broadband networks, proposing an Incentive-Based Spectrum Investment Framework (IBSIF) that would exchange longer spectrum certainty for measurable investments in network coverage, capacity, and service quality.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This report directly addresses the "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storyline, specifically highlighting the critical intersection of economic growth and infrastructure development in the telecommunications sector. The sheer scale of data revenue generated by MTN and Airtel signifies the digital transformation underway in Nigeria and the increasing reliance on mobile internet for various aspects of life, from digital payments and remote work to entertainment and cloud services. The N3.74 trillion figure is a testament to the market's dynamism. However, the accompanying surge in network congestion and subscriber complaints reveals a significant bottleneck. This mismatch between demand and infrastructure capacity is a classic challenge in rapidly developing economies.

The NCC's proposed shift in spectrum management strategy, moving from prioritizing immediate government revenue to using spectrum as leverage for long-term infrastructure development, is a crucial policy evolution. Extending license tenures to 15 years is a clear signal to operators that greater certainty in their operational environment will be tied to tangible investments in network upgrades. The IBSIF framework, which requires operators to demonstrate additional, measurable, and enforceable investments beyond their existing obligations, is designed to ensure that these incentives translate into real improvements in network quality and coverage, particularly in congested areas and underserved regions. The challenge will be in the effective implementation and monitoring of these commitments, ensuring that the investments are truly additional and lead to a better user experience for millions of Nigerians.
