### Atiku, Obi, CAN Criticize Tinubu's "Age of Prosperity" Claim on Independence Day
**Source:** Daily Trust / Punch / BusinessDay

**What happened**
On Nigeria's 66th Independence Anniversary, prominent political figures and a major civil society organization strongly challenged President Bola Tinubu's declaration that the nation had entered an "age of prosperity." Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, in his Independence Day address in Abuja, asserted that millions of Nigerians are still "struggling with food, transport, healthcare, education and insecurity." He directly questioned Tinubu's claim that "the 'emergency treatment is over' and the 'age of prosperity' has begun," stating, "A President may announce a new chapter in a speech. He cannot announce food onto a family’s table or money into a worker’s pocket. Bola, where is this prosperity?" Atiku provided concrete examples, noting that a ₦30,000 minimum wage in April 2023 could purchase approximately 118 litres of petrol, whereas a ₦70,000 minimum wage now buys only about 50 litres. He also highlighted the average price of an egg rising from ₦88 in April 2023 to ₦261 in May 2026, and a sliced loaf of bread increasing from ₦500 to ₦2,000 for a smaller size.

Similarly, Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), delivered a stark assessment, lamenting that over 140 million Nigerians are experiencing severe economic hardship, with more than 18 million children out of school. He warned that over 40 million Nigerians could face extreme hunger by the end of 2026 if urgent measures are not taken. The Christian Association of Nigeria (CAN), through its President, Archbishop Daniel Okoh, urged the Federal Government to reduce food, transport, and energy costs, strengthen social protection, and improve security. CAN emphasized that "the true test of progress is not in statistics alone. It is whether families can afford food, young people can find decent work, communities can live in safety, and every child has a fair opportunity to build a future," concluding that "prosperity that does not reach the ordinary Nigerian home remains incomplete."

**Deep Lore / Context**
This widespread and coordinated criticism from political opposition and civil society on such a symbolic national day marks a significant escalation in the ongoing "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline and directly challenges the government's narrative on "National Progress & Economy." For months, our briefings have tracked the severe economic fallout from the removal of fuel subsidies and the floating of the Naira, which have led to soaring inflation and a profound cost-of-living crisis. The "CBN Policy and Economic Management" storyline has consistently highlighted liquidity challenges, market volatility, and the growing threat of industrial action from federal workers over petrol prices – a threat that has now materialized into a nationwide strike. The opposition's detailed critiques, using specific price points and real-world impacts, aim to underscore a perceived disconnect between the government's official pronouncements and the daily struggles of ordinary Nigerians.

The convergence of voices from Atiku, Obi, and CAN amplifies the pressure on the Tinubu administration. This isn't just political rhetoric; it reflects deep-seated public discontent that has been simmering for months. The call for tangible relief and transparent social protection measures from CAN, a non-partisan moral voice, further legitimizes the public's grievances. As we approach the 2027 general elections, the government's ability to visibly improve the economic conditions for the average Nigerian will be crucial for its political legitimacy and stability. The current situation suggests that while economic reforms are underway, their benefits are yet to be felt by the majority, and the political cost of this disconnect is rapidly increasing.
