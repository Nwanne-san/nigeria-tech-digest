### Presidency Admits More Nigerians Fell into Poverty Under Tinubu
**Source:** Vanguard, Channels TV

The reforms, particularly the removal of fuel subsidies and the unification of exchange rates, have been widely cited as the primary drivers of increased cost of living and reduced purchasing power for average Nigerians. While the government frames this as a necessary, temporary phase for long-term economic stability and growth, the public's experience of "discomfort" is very real. This admission also implicitly responds to the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline, as it touches on public sentiment and presidential approval ahead of the 2027 elections, where the impact of economic policies will undoubtedly be a central issue. ### Igbokoda Crash: Keyamo, Air Chief Move to Strengthen Aviation Safety Cooperation
**Source:** BusinessDay

**What happened:** In the aftermath of the Nigerian Air Force (NAF) aircraft crash at Igbokoda, Ondo State, Festus Keyamo, the Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, and Air Chief Marshal Sunday Kelvin Aneke, the Chief of Air Staff, have agreed to enhance cooperation between Nigeria’s civil and military aviation authorities. Their agreement aims to establish a new framework that will cover critical areas such as airworthiness, certification, training, and overall safety protocols. Keyamo emphasized the shared responsibility, stating, "It is one country, one people. When disaster happens, it affects everybody." A joint committee involving the Nigerian Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) and NAF will be established to facilitate this collaboration, with NAF personnel also participating in relevant civil aviation training programmes.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a direct and significant follow-up to the "Security Operations and Insecurity Challenges (Nigeria)" storyline, which recently reported the tragic military aircraft crash that claimed the lives of 25 personnel and sparked questions about the adequacy of NAF aircraft maintenance funding. The move to deepen cooperation between civil and military aviation is a crucial step towards addressing systemic safety issues and bridging the historical operational divide between the two sectors. Such collaboration, particularly in areas like airworthiness and training, is vital for improving overall aviation safety standards, potentially preventing future tragedies, and ensuring more efficient use of resources and expertise. It reflects a governmental acknowledgment that national security and public safety require a unified, cross-sectoral approach, especially in critical infrastructure like aviation. This initiative aims to strengthen the country's capacity to manage and respond to aviation incidents, ultimately contributing to a more resilient national security framework.

The comparison to the Challenger disaster is a powerful metaphor for how incremental acceptance of risk can lead to catastrophic outcomes, intensifying global calls for more robust AI regulation and international accords. It highlights the ethical dilemma at the heart of AI development: balancing rapid innovation with the paramount need for safety and responsible deployment, especially as AI models gain more agency and impact real-world systems.
