### Biya’s Extended Absence Fuels Power Vacuum Debate in Neighboring Cameroon
**Headline:** Biya’s 48-day absence reignites debate over power vacuum in Cameroon (Punch)
**What happened:** Cameroon's 93-year-old President Paul Biya, the world's oldest head of state, has been absent from the country for 48 days, having departed on June 7 for a "short, private stay in Europe" with his wife, Chantal Biya. This prolonged absence has reignited concerns over a "glaring institutional vacuum," as described by Mamadou Mota, vice-president of the Cameroon Renaissance Movement (MRC). Despite a denial from Communication Minister Rene Emmanuel Sadi on June 18 that Biya was hospitalized, opposition parties like the Social Democratic Front (SDF) claim the country is "on autopilot." Calls for an "impartial procedure for determining the temporary or permanent incapacity" of the head of state have been made by Patricia Tomaino Ndam Njoya of the Cameroon Democratic Union (UDC).
**Deep Lore / Context:** President Biya has ruled Cameroon for over 40 years, and his frequent, extended trips abroad have historically fueled speculation about his health and the stability of his government. Allegations of a power vacuum are not new, with Biya having spent around 50 days outside the country in late 2024. This pattern of absence, coupled with unfulfilled promises (such as forming a new government team seven months after his December 31 address) and repeated postponements of municipal and legislative elections, has led to widespread frustration and a sense of government inaction among Cameroonians. The recent constitutional reform in May creating a vice-presidential post, which remains unfilled, further complicates the succession picture.

The current situation is amplifying internal power struggles within Biya's inner circle, with political scientist Stephane Akoa noting "increasingly leaks mention infighting between the various factions." First Lady Chantal Biya is also reportedly "increasingly present in state affairs," with her name frequently mentioned in decision-making. Without a vice-president, Senate President Aboubakary Abdoulaye is constitutionally next in line, but the uncertainty surrounding a post-Biya era is ripe for conspiracy and instability. For Nigeria, a stable Cameroon is crucial for regional security and economic cooperation, particularly given shared borders and the need for coordinated responses to cross-border challenges. Any significant political upheaval in a key neighbor could have ripple effects on Nigeria's own security and migration dynamics.
