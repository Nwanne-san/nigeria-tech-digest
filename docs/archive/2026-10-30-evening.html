Obasanjo Slams Tinubu Over Borrowing, Warns Against Foreign Economic Advice

Headline: Obasanjo: Nigeria takes economic instructions from experts who have never managed a household (Daily Trust)

What happened: Former President Olusegun Obasanjo delivered a scathing critique of President Bola Tinubu's economic policies and Nigeria's reliance on foreign economic advice. Speaking at the Ajibosin Platform in Abeokuta, Ogun State, themed "Leveraging Artificial Intelligence to solve grassroots problems and accelerate National Development," Obasanjo dismissed Tinubu's assertion that "borrowing is not leprosy" as "absolute nonsense." He argued that "Debt is worse than leprosy. Because leprosy destroys only one person. Debt destroys generations." He also questioned the capacity of foreign-trained "brilliant boys and girls" from Ivy League universities to provide relevant economic advice for a country of 250 million people, especially when they "are not even married, let alone manage a home." Obasanjo recalled that 47 years ago, the naira exchanged at N1 to $1.85, contrasting it sharply with the current N1,500 to the dollar, asking, "What has Nigeria done in 47 years that we now have 1,500 naira to the dollar? What have we done?"

Deep Lore / Context: This is a significant escalation in the ongoing storyline of National Progress & Economy (Nigeria), which we've been tracking as 'escalating'. Obasanjo's comments directly challenge the Tinubu administration's core economic strategy, which has increasingly leaned on foreign loans to fund ambitious infrastructure projects. Just yesterday, President Tinubu defended his administration's plan to take more foreign loans, arguing that Nigeria needs long-term investment in infrastructure to achieve its ambition of becoming a $1 trillion economy by 2030. He stated, "The key question is not how much we borrow, but what we build, the value we create and the future we leave behind." Obasanjo's intervention highlights a deep-seated philosophical divide within the Nigerian political elite regarding economic sovereignty and the efficacy of Western-prescribed economic models. His reference to the World Bank and IMF not being "designed for us" echoes historical debates about structural adjustment programs and their impact on developing nations. This isn't merely political grandstanding; it's a fundamental questioning of the current administration's approach to fiscal responsibility and national development, with the naira's continuing depreciation serving as a stark backdrop. The former president's emphasis on the long-term, generational impact of debt underscores the gravity of the nation's financial trajectory.

Beyond the borrowing debate, Obasanjo's remarks at the AI-themed forum also touch upon the developing storyline of African AI Sovereignty and Local Control. His warning against accepting foreign AI narratives without considering Nigeria's interests, drawing parallels to historical experiences of slave trade, colonialism, and structural adjustment, resonates deeply. He, along with guest speaker Dr. Umar Ardo, articulated a concern that unchecked data harvesting could become "the slave ship, refitted as the server farm," turning Africa into a "quarry" for data rather than an owner of intelligence. This perspective is crucial as Nigeria navigates the global AI landscape, emphasizing the need for local computing infrastructure, African-owned datasets, and directing AI towards grassroots governance. What happened: Major AI companies including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic have announced their adoption of a new standard that will allow publishers to opt out of having their content scraped for AI training. This means publishers can apply their AI training preferences across multiple AI companies simultaneously, rather than setting them individually.
