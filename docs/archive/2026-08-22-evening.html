Naira Ends Week with N11.12 Gain as FX Market Turnover Surges

Source: BusinessDay

What happened: The Naira concluded the week with a notable gain of N11.12 per dollar in the official foreign exchange (FX) market, driven by a surge in market liquidity and growing external buffers. Data from the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) showed the Naira appreciating by 0.83 percent week-on-week, with the dollar quoted at N1,346.49 on Friday, down from N1,357.61 a week prior. On a day-on-day basis, the local currency strengthened marginally by N1.14 from N1,347.63 on Thursday. In the parallel market, the Naira closed at N1,405, a 0.35 percent gain from N1,410, though the gap between official and parallel rates widened slightly to 4.75 percent. Total turnover at the Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market (NFEM) surged by 33.16 percent week-on-week to $5.26 billion by Thursday, up from $3.95 billion the previous week. Nigeria’s external reserves also maintained a steady growth, reaching $52.65 billion as of August 19, 2026, a 28.41 percent increase from $41.00 billion in the corresponding period of 2025.

Deep Lore / Context: This positive movement in the FX market is a crucial development in the CBN Policy and Economic Management and National Progress & Economy (Nigeria) storylines, both of which have been escalating. It follows earlier reports of the Naira strengthening against the dollar with increased FX turnover, signaling mixed but generally improving economic signals. The surge in market liquidity and the growth in external reserves provide the CBN with more "firepower" to support the Naira and meet external obligations, which is a key objective of the current administration's economic reforms. Finance Minister Taiwo Oyedele previously stated that without these reforms, the Naira could have traded between N550 and N800 to the dollar in 2026 but would have been largely unavailable, highlighting the perceived success of the current policies in stabilizing the currency. The reduction in the foreign exchange premium between the official and parallel markets from over 60 percent to less than 5 percent is also a significant achievement, aiming to reduce arbitrage and bring more transactions into official channels.

However, this positive FX performance exists alongside ongoing debates about the impact of reforms on public hardship, as highlighted by opposition figures like Atiku Abubakar and Donald Duke.
