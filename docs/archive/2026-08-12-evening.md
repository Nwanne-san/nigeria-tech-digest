### CBN Relaxes Discount Window Restrictions for FX and Government Securities
**Source:** Nairametrics, Premium Times

**What happened**
The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has announced the removal of restrictions that previously prevented financial institutions participating in the Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market (NFEM) and primary auctions of government securities from accessing its Discount Window. This policy change, effective immediately, was communicated via a circular signed by Okey Umeano, the CBN’s Acting Director, Financial Markets Department. The review follows an assessment of market developments and existing practices in the foreign exchange, money, and fixed-income markets. Additionally, the CBN has lifted the suspension of Tenored Repo Operations, which will now range from 4 to 90 days to support liquidity management and enhance monetary policy. The OMO participation framework has also been revised to allow a broader range of investors, including individuals, corporates, and non-bank financial institutions, to participate in both primary and secondary OMO markets through Deposit Money Banks (DMBs).

**Deep Lore / Context**
This is a significant and positive shift in the "CBN Policy and Economic Management" storyline, which has been "escalating" due to ongoing efforts to manage economic stability and FX liquidity amidst high interest rates and tight lending. The removal of these restrictions is a crucial step towards improving liquidity in the financial system and fostering greater flexibility in money market operations. Previously, banks faced a trade-off: participate in the FX market or government securities auctions, or access the CBN's Standing Lending Facility (SLF) for short-term liquidity. This restriction often created market rigidities and could exacerbate liquidity squeezes. By removing it, the CBN aims to encourage more active participation across these critical markets without penalising institutions for seeking necessary liquidity.

The lifting of the suspension on Tenored Repo Operations further enhances the CBN's toolkit for liquidity management, allowing for more nuanced and flexible interventions in the money market. This is particularly important in an environment where the CBN maintains monetary discipline to combat inflation and stabilise the Naira. The expansion of OMO participation to a broader range of investors, beyond just DMBs, is also a notable move. It aims to deepen the financial markets, increase participation, and potentially improve the transmission mechanism of monetary policy. This policy adjustment reflects the CBN's commitment to refining its tools to achieve economic stability, particularly in managing FX liquidity, which has been a persistent challenge impacting various sectors of the economy, including the tech ecosystem's access to foreign currency for operations and imports.

### Anthropic's "Dreaming" Agents and the Maintainability Challenge of AI-Generated Code
**Source:** The New Stack

**What happened**
Anthropic has introduced "dreaming," an asynchronous, out-of-band process for its AI agents to autonomously manage and curate their memory systems. Lamis Mukta, a member of technical staff at Anthropic, explained that dreaming consolidates memory, removes irrelevant information, adds missing elements, and cleans up memory systems, running in batch with allocated resources. This process aims to help agents learn over time by spotting recurring failure patterns and proposing memory-store updates for human review. However, experts like Jayakumar Ramalingam warn that "dreaming" could create a "dangerous promotion path" where repeated mistakes lead to persistent policy, noting that "a bad answer normally dies with the session; a bad memory can influence thousands of future sessions." He advocates for memory to carry provenance, evidence, and an expiration condition, viewing memory as a state management problem rather than just a context window. Separately, a June 2026 study, "Is Agent Code Less Maintainable Than Human Code?", found that coding agents were less successful (up to 13.1% difference in task-resolution rates) when building on code previously written by another agent, even if the initial code passed all tests. Human reviewers also required 11.8% more review rounds for agent-written code, and AI review suggestions led to larger increases in code size and complexity.

**Deep Lore / Context**
These two stories collectively represent a significant escalation and deepening of the "AI Agents in Enterprise Observability," "AI Security Vulnerabilities," and "AI Governance and Regulation Gap" storylines. Anthropic's "dreaming" concept is an innovative attempt to address the critical challenge of agent memory management, which is crucial for agents to perform complex, long-running tasks effectively. By allowing agents to self-curate their knowledge base, Anthropic aims to overcome the limitations of static context windows and human-driven memory updates. This directly relates to the proliferation of AI agents and the investment in supporting agentic application architectures. However, the expert critiques highlight profound security and governance implications: the risk of "poisoned memory" or "bad answers" becoming persistent policy through automated processes, underscoring the urgent need for robust auditing, versioning, and human oversight in agent systems. This echoes the "Zoomsday" hack and vulnerabilities allowing LLM reasoning theft, intensifying calls for robust regulation and independent audits.

The research on AI-generated code maintainability further compounds these concerns. This challenges the notion of AI agents as pure productivity boosters and emphasizes the ongoing critical role of human engineers in ensuring the reliability, security, and long-term viability of software systems. This directly connects to the "productivity paradox" mentioned in "AI Model Competition and Efficiency," where AI speeds up coding but doesn't necessarily increase overall engineering velocity if maintainability issues create downstream bottlenecks.
