### AI Impersonation Could Become Nigeria’s Biggest Banking Fraud Threat in 2027
**Source:** BusinessDay

**What happened:** Samuel Ubido, executive chairman of Techinnovate Group, issued a stark warning that artificial intelligence-powered impersonation is poised to become Nigeria’s most significant banking fraud threat by 2027. He explained that criminals are increasingly exploiting cloned voices, manipulated videos, and convincing digital identities to trick both customers and financial institution employees into authorizing fraudulent transactions. Ubido highlighted that the growing sophistication of AI-assisted scams, combined with Nigeria’s instant payment infrastructure, could expose banks and fintech companies to a new generation of fraud that bypasses conventional security systems. He noted that fraudsters may no longer need to breach banking systems or steal passwords; instead, they can manipulate individuals to approve payments, making these transactions appear legitimate. Ubido cited documented cases in Africa, including a January 2026 investment scam that used a doctored video of Aliko Dangote and a South African deepfake involving rugby captain Siya Kolisi. He recommended that banks strengthen real-time fraud detection by combining multiple risk indicators, improve authentication systems beyond PINs and OTPs, and for the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) to establish a coordinated, round-the-clock fraud intelligence sharing mechanism among financial institutions.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This warning is a critical escalation within the "Adversarial AI Evolution and Cybersecurity" storyline, which has been "escalating" by noting how AI accelerates cyber threats and introduces new attack vectors. It provides a concrete, near-term projection of how these advanced capabilities will manifest as a direct threat to Nigeria's financial sector. The "Fintech Diversification and Business Infrastructure" storyline, which is "developing," highlights the rapid growth and innovation in Nigeria's digital payment ecosystem. While this has driven financial inclusion and convenience, it also creates a fertile ground for sophisticated fraud, as the speed of transactions allows stolen funds to be moved rapidly across institutions, leaving little time for detection and recovery. The existing "CBN Policy and Economic Management" storyline, which is "escalating," has focused on fiscal and monetary policy, but this new threat underscores the urgent need for the CBN to also prioritize cybersecurity and fraud prevention within its regulatory purview, particularly as it advances its Payments System Vision 2028.

The shift from system penetration to human manipulation through AI-generated content represents a paradigm change in cybersecurity. Traditional security measures, such as transaction alerts and one-time passwords, are rendered less effective when the victim is coerced into voluntarily disclosing credentials or approving fraudulent transactions. This necessitates a fundamental re-evaluation of security architectures, moving towards more robust behavioral analytics, biometric verification, and AI-driven anomaly detection that can discern genuine intent from manipulation. The call for a CBN-led, interbank fraud intelligence sharing platform is crucial, as the interconnectedness of Nigeria's payment system means a vulnerability in one institution can quickly propagate across the entire ecosystem. This development signifies that the battle against cybercrime will increasingly be fought with AI on both sides, demanding a rapid upskilling and investment in defensive AI capabilities within the Nigerian financial and tech sectors.
