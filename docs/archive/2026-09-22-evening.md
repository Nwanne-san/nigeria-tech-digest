### Tinubu’s Prolonged Absence Ignites Constitutional Debate and Opposition Outcry
**Source:** Daily Trust, Punch, Channels TV, Premium Times, Vanguard

**What happened:** President Bola Tinubu's extended "working vacation" in Europe, now stretching beyond three weeks, has ignited a significant constitutional debate across Nigeria. Senate President Godswill Akpabio, speaking in Akwa Ibom State, staunchly defended Tinubu, insisting there is "no vacancy in Aso Rock" and that the President is still effectively running Nigeria from abroad, citing the enabling power of technological advancements. However, this stance drew sharp criticism from the African Democratic Congress (ADC). Mallam Bolaji Abdullahi, the party's National Publicity Secretary, labeled Tinubu's continued absence a "national disgrace," particularly noting that French President Emmanuel Macron, with whom Tinubu recently held bilateral talks, was actively attending the United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) in New York, while Vice President Kashim Shettima represented Nigeria. Abdullahi pointedly questioned who currently exercises constitutional presidential powers, demanding clarity on whether the required declaration under Section 145 of the 1999 Constitution was transmitted, given Tinubu's absence now exceeds 21 days. Adding to the discourse, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde, also a presidential candidate for 2027, pledged to strictly adhere to Section 145 by transmitting power to his deputy when on vacation for more than 21 days, and committed to doing so if elected president.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is the third major escalation this month in the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline, which has been tracking concerns over leadership absence and the potential for constitutional breaches. The debate around Section 145 is a recurring theme in Nigerian political history, resurfacing whenever a president is abroad for an extended period without formally transferring power. Historically, the argument from the Presidency has often been that modern communication allows for governance from any location. However, opposition figures and constitutional purists argue that the intent of Section 145 is to prevent a leadership vacuum, ensure transparency, and uphold the principle of accountability, especially in a nation grappling with complex challenges. The current administration's stance, reinforced by Senate President Akpabio, suggests a continued reliance on informal arrangements rather than strict constitutional adherence, which the opposition views as a dangerous precedent.

The political implications are significant, particularly with the 2027 general election cycle already casting a long shadow. Opposition parties, like the ADC and figures like Governor Makinde, are strategically using this issue to critique the incumbent administration's governance style and position themselves as champions of constitutionalism and good governance. Governor Makinde's explicit pledge to follow Section 145, while seemingly a statement of principle, also serves as a subtle but clear contrast to the current situation, aiming to resonate with a populace increasingly concerned about leadership transparency and accountability. The public's perception of leadership presence and the rule of law is a critical factor, and this ongoing debate could shape the narratives and electoral strategies of various political actors as the nation moves towards the next election.

### CBN Cuts MPR by 350bps, Foreign Reserves Surge to $55 Billion Amid Inflation Concerns
**Source:** Nairametrics, Premium Times, Daily Trust

**What happened:** The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has implemented a significant cut in its Monetary Policy Rate (MPR), reducing it by 350 basis points from 26.5% to 23%. CBN Governor Olayemi Cardoso explained that this "reset" was necessary because the previous benchmark had become "disconnected from the rates actually used in the financial markets." This policy adjustment is anticipated to "drive equities demand as investors rotate from fixed income." In a positive economic indicator, Governor Cardoso also announced that Nigeria's foreign reserves have surpassed $55 billion, marking their highest level in over 18 years, a development he attributed to the current CBN management's success in ensuring monetary and price stability. Despite these encouraging signs, the CBN issued a warning that "prolonged geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and election-related spending" could potentially disrupt the ongoing disinflation trend. The latest figures show headline inflation slowing to 15.39% in August 2026 from 15.43% in July, with food inflation also declining to 19.57% from 20.31% over the same period, reflecting the impact of previous monetary tightening, sustained exchange-rate stability, and improved food supply.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a major development in the "CBN Policy and Economic Management" storyline, which has been tracking the CBN's efforts to strengthen fiscal-monetary policy coordination amidst global economic pressures and rising domestic costs. The MPR cut, following a period of aggressive tightening, signals a potential pivot in the CBN's strategy. While still committed to price stability, this move suggests a greater emphasis on stimulating economic growth by lowering borrowing costs for businesses and encouraging investment. The previous high MPR had made fixed income investments highly attractive, drawing capital away from productive sectors and the equities market. By reducing the MPR, the CBN aims to rebalance this, making equities more appealing and potentially fostering a more dynamic investment environment. The surge in foreign reserves to $55 billion, the highest in over 18 years, is a strong indicator of improved investor confidence and potentially successful foreign exchange market interventions, directly supporting the "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storyline, which has noted the nation's economic challenges but also pockets of progress.

However, the CBN's cautionary note about external and domestic risks is critical. "Prolonged geopolitical tensions in the Middle East," referencing the ongoing conflicts involving the US, Israel, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and Yemen, could disrupt global oil supplies and drive up prices. As an oil-dependent economy, Nigeria would face increased import costs and inflationary pressures from such a scenario. The "election-related spending" ahead of the 2027 general election is a perennial concern in Nigeria, often leading to increased liquidity, speculative activities, and inflationary pressures. The delicate balance for the CBN is to manage these external shocks and domestic political cycles while fostering sustainable growth. The observed moderation in headline, food, and core inflation in August provides some justification for the MPR cut, but the path to sustained disinflation remains precarious and subject to these significant external and internal variables.

### AI Coding Agents Pose New Cybersecurity Risks, Demanding Secrets-Safe Context Boundaries
**Source:** The New Stack, Ars Technica

**What happened:** While AI coding agents offer immense benefits for software development, they also introduce critical new cybersecurity vulnerabilities, specifically the risk of "secrets leaking to AI coding agents and becoming model context." These agents, in their inherent quest for comprehensive context to perform tasks like bug investigation or refactoring, may inadvertently read sensitive local files such as `.env` files, cloud credential profiles, SSH configurations, or application logs. This creates a "quiet path from local files to external systems," where credentials can be transmitted to external AI services, bypassing traditional security checkpoints like code commits and pull requests. The urgency of this threat was underscored by Microsoft's recent disruption of "EvilTokens," an "AI-assisted platform" that had compromised 12,000 entities, demonstrating the escalating sophistication of AI-driven cyberattacks. Security experts are now emphasizing the need to treat agent context as an "egress surface" and implement a zero-trust approach. This involves deterministic controls that block or redact sensitive material before it is ever sent to an AI model, as "asking an LLM to decide whether to transmit a credential does not create a reliable security boundary." Instead, purpose-built secrets detection, integrated directly into IDEs, command lines, and pre-submission workflows, is deemed essential.
