### Senate Urges INEC to Disqualify Candidates Over Electoral Violence
**Headline** (Vanguard): Senate urges INEC to disqualify candidates over violence
**What happened**
The Nigerian Senate has called on the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to disqualify political candidates who are found, through established court convictions, to have instigated or participated in electoral violence. This resolution, adopted after a motion by Senator Abdul Ningi (Bauchi Central), also urged state governors to cease denying opposition parties access to public facilities like hotels and stadia for political activities. The Senate condemned the increasing frequency and spread of political violence across the country, highlighting incidents in Abuja, Edo, Delta, and Sokoto states, including an attack on President Bola Tinubu’s motorcade and former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar’s convoy. Senate President Godswill Akpabio emphasized that Sections 121 to 128 of the Electoral Act 2026 prescribe penalties from 12 months to five years’ imprisonment for electoral violence, and Section 92 empowers INEC to suspend and reschedule elections in affected areas.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This is a significant escalation in the ongoing storyline of **Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)**. The early campaign activities for the 2027 elections have been consistently marked by reported political violence, internal party disputes, and strategic maneuvering, underscoring persistent challenges to electoral fairness and democratic processes. The Senate's intervention reflects a growing concern among lawmakers about the integrity of the upcoming elections, following a pattern of pre-election violence that has plagued Nigeria's democratic history. The call for disqualification, if enforced, would represent a strong deterrent, but its effectiveness hinges on the independence and impartiality of the judiciary and security agencies, which have historically faced accusations of partisanship. The push for state police, a recurring debate, could also become a significant political factor in controlling or exacerbating such localized violence, depending on its implementation and oversight.

The issue of political violence and the denial of public facilities to opposition parties directly undermines the principle of a level playing field, a cornerstone of democratic elections. Past elections have seen similar allegations, leading to reduced voter turnout and questions about the legitimacy of outcomes. The Electoral Offences Commission Bill 2025, passed alongside the Electoral Act, was intended to provide a dedicated mechanism for addressing electoral offenses, including vote-buying and attacks on electoral officials. However, the Senate's current resolution suggests that existing legal frameworks and institutions are still perceived as insufficient or inadequately enforced. The focus now shifts to INEC and security agencies to demonstrate their capacity and willingness to implement these recommendations impartially, particularly as the 2027 election cycle intensifies.

### External Sandboxes for Coding Agents Enhance Safety
**Headline** (Simon Willison (AI)): External sandboxes for coding agents
**What happened**
Simon Willison, in collaboration with AI research lab Prime Radiant, has been exploring and releasing new methods to sandbox code executed by AI agents, with a focus on enhancing safety for local models. This "external sandbox pattern" involves running agent-executed code within a dedicated sandbox, separate from the agent itself, to prevent damaging actions from mistakes or prompt injection attacks. Three sandbox server implementations have been released: `external-sandbox-v86` (Node.js server running Alpine Linux in WebAssembly), `external-sandbox-container` (Python web server running Alpine Linux in Apple Containers), and `external-sandbox-quicksand` (Python web server using Microsoft's Quicksand library with QEMU and Alpine Linux). These implementations offer a consistent localhost API, allowing agents like Pi, Claude Code, or Codex to send API calls for code execution. They also include an optional HTTP/HTTPS allow-list proxy and a credential replacement mechanism for secure API calls without exposing secrets to the agent.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This development is a critical advancement in the escalating **Adversarial AI Evolution and Cybersecurity** storyline and directly addresses concerns within the **AI Governance and Regulation Gap** storyline. As AI models become more agentic and capable of executing code, the risks of unintended consequences, security vulnerabilities, and malicious exploitation (e.g., through prompt injection) grow exponentially. The "external sandbox pattern" is a fundamental security primitive designed to contain these risks, ensuring that even if an AI agent makes an error or is compromised, its actions are confined to a controlled environment, preventing damage to the host system or unauthorized access to sensitive data. This approach is crucial for fostering trust and enabling the safe deployment of increasingly powerful AI agents in real-world applications.

The release of multiple sandbox implementations, leveraging diverse technologies like WebAssembly, Apple Containers, and QEMU, demonstrates the active and collaborative effort within the tech community to build robust safety mechanisms. The inclusion of features like an HTTP/HTTPS allow-list proxy and credential replacement further strengthens the security posture, allowing developers fine-grained control over an agent's network access and preventing the leakage of sensitive API keys. This focus on verifiable AI solutions and machine-speed defense mechanisms is essential as AI models are also being developed for offensive cybersecurity capabilities. The work by Simon Willison and Prime Radiant is a practical step towards establishing robust guardrails for AI, contributing to the broader debate on responsible AI development and the urgent need for policy frameworks to manage AI's societal impact.
