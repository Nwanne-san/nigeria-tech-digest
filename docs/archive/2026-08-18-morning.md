### PANDEF Seeks Power Devolution, Return to Fiscal Federalism
**Source:** Daily Trust
**What happened:** The Pan Niger Delta Forum (PANDEF), through its National Chairman Ambassador Godknows Igali, has called for a return to true fiscal federalism and power devolution. Speaking at the Concerned Citizens Assembly summit in Abuja, Igali argued that Nigeria's current over-centralised system has weakened productivity, security, and accountability. The summit, organised by the Citizens Rebirth and Awareness Initiative (C-RAIN), proposed the creation of 15 regions as part of a broader restructuring framework. Igali criticised state governors for failing to translate regional cooperation into economic development and urged constitutional amendments to devolve powers, strengthen fiscal federalism, restore greater resource control, and guarantee local government autonomy.
**Deep Lore / Context:** This call for fiscal federalism is a long-standing demand, particularly from resource-rich regions like the Niger Delta, which feel short-changed by the current revenue allocation formula. The historical context harks back to Nigeria's First Republic, where regions like the Western Region (free primary education, Cocoa House), Eastern Region (industries), and Northern Region (agriculture, educational institutions) developed largely based on their own resources and priorities, contributing to a "limited but effective centre." This period is often romanticised as a golden age of regional development, contrasting sharply with the current system where states are heavily reliant on monthly allocations from the Federal Government. The argument is that this over-centralisation stifles innovation, breeds inefficiency, and exacerbates inter-state and inter-ethnic tensions as states compete for federal handouts rather than developing their unique economic strengths.

The escalation history of this issue shows a consistent push for restructuring, often intensifying during periods of economic strain or political dissatisfaction. While previous administrations have largely resisted significant restructuring, the current economic challenges and widespread insecurity are lending new urgency to these calls. The proposal for 15 regions, a concrete framework emerging from consultations across all six geopolitical zones, signals a more organised and detailed approach from proponents. This move connects directly to ongoing debates about national unity and the effectiveness of governance, suggesting that without fundamental changes to the architecture of power, Nigeria's complex and diverse federation will continue to struggle with economic performance and cohesion. Watch today for any official responses or further discussions from political figures or other regional groups on these proposals, as they could signal a shift in the national conversation.

### Anthropic Defined the Standards Inside Agent Plugins. So Why Isn’t It Helping Govern the Format?
**Source:** The New Stack
**What happened:** Vercel released Agent Plugins 1.0.0 with core maintainers from AWS, Cursor, Microsoft, and OpenAI, with Google joining them. This format aims to fix interoperability issues for developers by standardising where components live. However, Anthropic, which defined the underlying Agent Skills and MCP (Multi-Component Protocol) standards and released them openly, is notably absent from the Agent Plugins Technical Steering Committee. This absence means the Agent Plugins project lacks authority over the very standards it relies on, leading to a "small interoperability floor" where terms like "compatible" and "portable" suggest more than the narrow agreement actually provides.
**Deep Lore / Context:** This story is a critical development for the "AI Governance and Regulation Gap" and "AI Agents in Enterprise Observability" storylines, highlighting the complexities and challenges of standardisation in the rapidly evolving AI ecosystem. Anthropic's decision to open-source its foundational standards (MCP to the Agentic AI Foundation, and Agent Skills as an open specification) was a move towards promoting broader adoption and interoperability. However, its absence from the governance of Agent Plugins, which leverages these standards, creates a significant "governance gap." This situation exemplifies the tension between open innovation and controlled standardisation in a competitive market.

The core issue is that while Agent Plugins standardises *locations* (e.g., `skills/`, `mcp.json`), it "declines to" constrain the *behavioral implementations* of Agent Skills and MCP. This means that a client conforming to Agent Plugins might only support skills, or only MCP, or only specific transports, leading to a wide "range of things a conformance claim can mean." As the article notes, "The bare claim of Agent Plugins conformance does not predict interoperability." This fragmentation in standards and governance can hinder the widespread adoption and seamless integration of AI agents, which are becoming increasingly important for enterprise operations. For you, as a developer, this underscores the importance of understanding the nuances of "interoperability" claims and the need to build flexible, adaptable systems. *   **AI Hardware Race & Market Consolidation:** The massive Nvidia investment in OpenAI's data center signals that the AI hardware race will continue to escalate, with dominant players like Nvidia solidifying their positions. *   **AI Security as a Top Priority:** The alarming increase in AI-enabled cyber breaches and the launch of AI-powered defensive platforms like DEPAS AI indicate that AI security will rapidly become a paramount concern for enterprises and governments. Expect to see significant R&D investment in AI-driven threat detection, autonomous security agents, and robust AI model auditing. Regulatory bodies may also start mandating AI security standards, creating a new compliance landscape.

*   **Interoperability Challenges for AI Agents:** The governance gap in Agent Plugins, despite Anthropic's foundational work, suggests that true interoperability for AI agents will remain a significant challenge in the near term. While efforts to standardise will continue, expect a fragmented ecosystem with various proprietary and semi-open standards. Developers will need to be adept at working with multiple APIs and frameworks, and the market for "translation layers" or integration platforms will likely grow.

*   **Nigerian SME AI Adoption: A Gradual Ascent:** The proposed six-step AI roadmap for Nigerian SMEs is pragmatic, but widespread adoption will be a gradual process. The persistent challenges of inadequate digital skills, poor electricity, and unreliable internet connectivity will act as significant brakes. Government incentives and robust infrastructure development (like the rural telecom project) will be crucial, but local tech solutions that are low-cost, offline-capable, and culturally relevant will be key to overcoming these hurdles.

*   **Fiscal Federalism Debate Intensifies:** The calls for power devolution and fiscal federalism from groups like PANDEF are likely to intensify, especially as economic pressures continue and the 2027 elections draw closer. While immediate constitutional amendments are complex, the debate will force a re-evaluation of Nigeria's governance structure. This could lead to incremental policy shifts towards greater state autonomy in resource management and revenue generation, potentially unlocking new regional economic development models.

*   **Naira Stability and Economic Reforms:** The recent strengthening of the naira is a positive sign, but its sustainability remains speculative. It likely reflects a combination of CBN interventions and perhaps a temporary increase in foreign currency inflows. The long-term stability of the naira and the broader economy will depend on the government's commitment to structural reforms, including addressing fuel subsidy costs, boosting local production, and attracting sustained foreign direct investment, rather than just portfolio investments. Expect continued volatility but with a hopeful outlook for gradual improvement if policies remain consistent.
