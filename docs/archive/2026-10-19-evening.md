### LLM Evaluators Are Subjective and Biased, Requiring Auditing for Reliability
**Headline:** Who judges the judge? Three helpful ways to audit your LLM evaluators (Google Cloud Blog)
**What happened:** Google Cloud's Senior Technical Solutions Consultant, Héctor González Álvarez, published a post challenging the misconception that AI models provide objective evaluation. He asserts that LLM judges are subjective and carry biases such as "verbosity bias" (favoring longer answers), "position bias" (flipping verdicts based on option order over 75% of the time), "halo effect" (clean grammar masking factual errors), "agreeableness bias" (failing to catch errors up to 75% of the time), and "self-enhancement bias" (overrating their own outputs). To improve quality, he proposes a three-pillar method: measuring internal stability with multi-pass consistency checks, validating human alignment using chance-corrected metrics, and employing multi-judge ensembles with decomposed prompting to isolate evaluation criteria.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This article is a critical contribution to the "AI Model Competition and Enterprise Adoption" and "AI Governance and Regulation Gap" storylines, both of which are escalating.
