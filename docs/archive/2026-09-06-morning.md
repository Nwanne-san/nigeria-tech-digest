### 1. Nigerians Question Country’s Economic Growth Figures Amid Growing Hardship
**Source:** BusinessDay
**What happened:** Nigeria’s National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) reported a real GDP growth of 4.43% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, an increase from 4.23% in Q2 2025 and 3.89% in Q1 2026. The Federal Government hailed these figures as proof that its economic reforms are yielding results, positioning Nigeria stronger for its $1 trillion economy target by 2030. However, this official optimism is sharply contrasted by widespread public skepticism, with many Nigerians questioning what this growth means for their daily lives amidst rising food costs, declining purchasing power, and high petrol prices. The NBS Cost of a Healthy Diet report indicated an average daily cost of N1,541 per adult in March 2026, up 4.4% from N1,477 in March 2025. Shehu Mohammed, President of the Association of Senior Civil Servants of Nigeria (ASCSN), stated that the N70,000 minimum wage has already lost significant value to inflation, while Festus Osifo, President of the Trade Union Congress (TUC), called for remuneration in the oil and gas industry to reflect prevailing inflation. An ordinary Nigerian, Adebukola Janet, lamented that the hardship has become so severe that people can no longer support those they once helped.

**Deep Lore / Context:**
This story directly follows our ongoing storyline, "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)," which has been escalating due to persistent economic challenges like rising costs and brain drain, despite government efforts in trade expansion and corporate stability.
