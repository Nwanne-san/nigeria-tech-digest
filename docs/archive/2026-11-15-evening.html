SERAP Demands Prosecution of Electoral Violence Suspects, Citing 2027 Campaign Attacks

Source: Channels TV

What happened The Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project (SERAP) has issued a seven-day ultimatum to the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), demanding the prosecution of individuals arrested for electoral and political violence related to the ongoing 2027 general elections campaign. In a letter dated November 14, 2026, addressed to INEC Chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, SERAP urged the commission to obtain case files from the Nigeria Police Force and initiate prosecutions under the Electoral Act 2026. The demand follows numerous reported incidents since campaigns began on August 19, including a recent attack on Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) members in Oregun, Lagos, on November 12. Other cited incidents include attacks on NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi's convoy in Benue (September 8) and Calabar (November 4), attacks on political meetings in Enugu (September 12), and an attack on an NDC procession in Asaba (November 7) which reportedly left one person dead and five injured.

Deep Lore / Context This is a direct and significant escalation in the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline, which we've been tracking as "escalating" due to early campaign activities marked by reported political violence and strategic maneuvering. Electoral violence has been a persistent and corrosive element in Nigeria's democratic journey, undermining the credibility and fairness of elections. Historically, the failure to prosecute electoral offenders has created a culture of impunity, where perpetrators and their sponsors often escape justice, emboldening further violence. SERAP's intervention highlights this systemic weakness, pushing INEC to exercise its statutory powers under Section 148(2) of the Electoral Act 2026, which mandates the commission's legal officers or appointed practitioners to undertake such prosecutions. The repeated incidents across various states and against different political parties, including the NDC, ADC, and AAC, underscore the widespread nature of the problem, suggesting a coordinated effort by some actors to suppress political opposition and intimidate voters.

The implications of INEC's response to this ultimatum are profound. If the commission fails to act decisively, it risks further eroding public trust in the electoral process and discouraging citizen participation, particularly among vulnerable groups and opposition supporters. For the broader Nigerian society, a climate of political violence translates into instability, fear, and a diversion of resources that could otherwise be channeled into development. For the tech ecosystem, a stable and predictable political environment is a prerequisite for sustained growth and investment. When political processes are marred by violence and impunity, it introduces significant uncertainty, deters foreign capital, and can even lead to talent flight, as individuals seek safer and more stable environments. The call for accountability is not just about justice for victims but about safeguarding the foundational principles upon which a modern, thriving economy and society can be built.

Tinubu’s Government Pays N1.23 Trillion of Power Sector Legacy Debts to Revive Electricity Supply

Source: BusinessDay

What happened President Bola Tinubu’s government has paid N1.23 trillion of the N3.3 trillion legacy debts owed to power generation companies (GenCos), according to Power Minister Joseph Tegbe. This payment, representing over a third of the verified obligations, is a strategic move aimed at reviving Nigeria's dysfunctional electricity sector. Tegbe acknowledged public frustration with unreliable supply, attributing the sector's troubles to systemic issues including gas supply constraints, ageing infrastructure, vandalism, transmission bottlenecks, poor metering, and commercial weaknesses across the entire value chain. The government is also undertaking grid upgrades, commissioning new substations adding over 1,300 megavolt-amperes (MVA) to transmission capacity, building a new National Grid Control Centre in Osogbo, and working towards expanding generation capacity to 10,000 megawatts. Additionally, more than 2.8 million customers were metered between 2023 and October 2026 under the Presidential Metering Initiative.

Deep Lore / Context This is a critical development in the "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storyline, which has consistently highlighted energy infrastructure deficits as a major impediment to economic growth. The issue of unpaid bills to GenCos has been a chronic problem since the privatization of the power sector in 2013, creating a vicious cycle where power plants struggle to purchase gas, maintain equipment, and attract new investment, thereby limiting electricity supply. This N1.23 trillion payment, while substantial, still leaves N2.1 trillion in approved payments outstanding, indicating the immense scale of the legacy debt burden. The minister's comprehensive diagnosis of systemic issues underscores that a holistic approach, beyond just debt payment, is required to fix the sector. The metering initiative and grid upgrades are long-standing efforts to address inefficiencies, reduce aggregate technical, commercial, and collection losses, and improve revenue collection, which is vital for the sector's solvency.

The government's bet is that clearing these arrears will break the cycle of dysfunction and attract the necessary investment to stabilize and expand power supply. A reliable and adequate power supply is foundational for any modern economy, directly impacting industrial productivity, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), and the quality of life for citizens. For the tech developer, improved power means a significant reduction in operational costs associated with running generators, more reliable internet infrastructure, and a more conducive environment for data centers, cloud services, and remote work. It directly supports the growth of the digital economy and innovation. The success of this initiative will be a key determinant of Nigeria's overall economic recovery and its ability to compete in the global digital landscape, making it a story to watch closely.
