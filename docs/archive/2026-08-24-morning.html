  • Headline: Nigeria's unclaimed dividend and Tinubu's dangerous dip into Capital Market waters (BusinessDay)
  • What happened: President Bola Tinubu has directed that unclaimed dividends, currently held within the Unclaimed Funds Trust Fund (UFTF), be redirected to shore up the Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND).   • Deep Lore / Context: This action taps into a substantial pool of funds, estimated to be over ₦242 billion in unclaimed dividends. The Independent Shareholders Association of Nigeria has previously labelled similar proposals as a \"gross violation of shareholders' rights\" and a \"dangerous precedent.\" The argument is that repurposing these funds, even for a noble cause like education financing, suggests that capital market structures are susceptible to fiscal pressures, potentially eroding investor confidence. This is particularly critical at a time when Nigeria is actively seeking foreign direct investment and capital deepening. The article suggests that instead of diverting these funds, the government should focus on addressing the systemic issues that lead to such a large volume of unclaimed dividends, such as improving the e-dividend registration process and simplifying probate requirements.

  • The "AI Slop" Problem Persists: Despite user pushback, the proliferation of low-quality, AI-generated content is likely to continue, posing challenges for information integrity. This will increase the demand for tools and expertise in AI content verification and curation.

  • Enterprise AI Adoption Becomes More Pragmatic: Businesses will move beyond hype to focus on practical AI applications that demonstrate clear ROI. This will drive demand for AI engineers who can integrate AI solutions into existing workflows and solve specific business problems, rather than just building theoretical models.

  • Geopolitical Factors Influence AI Hardware Access: The global competition for AI hardware, coupled with geopolitical tensions, could impact the availability and cost of essential components for AI development, potentially influencing Nigeria's ability to scale its AI infrastructure.
