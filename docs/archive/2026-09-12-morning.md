### FG Settles N18 Billion Severance for 2,100 Former Nigeria Airways Workers After Two Decades
**Source:** Premium Times, Nairametrics

**What happened**
The Federal Government has commenced the payment of an N18 billion severance settlement to 2,700 former workers of the defunct Nigeria Airways Limited, more than two decades after the national carrier was liquidated in 2003. As of Friday, September 12, 2026, 2,100 former workers in Batches 1 to 7 have already received their benefits, with payments for an additional 600 beneficiaries in Batches 8 and 9 being finalized and expected within days. Finance Minister and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, Taiwo Oyedele, stated that this exercise reflects the government's determination to address legitimate outstanding obligations. A second-phase mop-up exercise is planned for beneficiaries whose records could not be processed initially, including deceased beneficiaries whose next of kin or estates will undergo verification.

**Deep Lore / Context**
This development marks a significant, albeit long-overdue, step in resolving a protracted issue that has plagued thousands of Nigerian families for over two decades. The liquidation of Nigeria Airways in 2003 left thousands of employees, including pilots, engineers, cabin crew, and administrative staff, without their pensions, gratuities, and other entitlements. This created immense hardship, with many former workers passing away before receiving their dues. The issue has been a persistent point of contention, leading to numerous protests, petitions, and threats of industrial action by aviation unions over the years. Previous administrations made partial payments, but the full settlement remained elusive, highlighting systemic issues in government's handling of employee welfare and post-privatization liabilities.

The current administration's decision to finalize this payment, following President Bola Tinubu's approval in July 2025 for long-awaited pensions to retired aviation workers, signals a commitment to addressing historical injustices and restoring public trust. This action, coming amidst broader economic reforms and a focus on fiscal responsibility, could be seen as an effort to clear the slate and demonstrate good governance. It also provides a stark contrast to the ongoing debates about the future of a national flag carrier, especially after the indefinite suspension of the Nigeria Air project in May 2024. The government is now reportedly considering supporting existing local airlines to emerge as Nigerian flag carriers, a different approach to developing the aviation sector, which this settlement might indirectly support by resolving past liabilities and potentially freeing up focus for future initiatives.
