National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)

CBN Cuts T-Bill Rate Amidst Strong Investor Demand for Long-Term Securities The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has lowered the stop rate on the 364-day Treasury Bill (T-Bill) to 17.15%, a decrease of 44 basis points from the previous auction. This occurred despite overwhelming investor demand, with N3.63 trillion submitted for the one-year instrument, representing 95.9% of total bids. Investors are increasingly favoring longer-term returns in Nigeria's fixed-income market, with demand for shorter-tenor instruments remaining subdued. The strong demand for the 364-day bill allowed the CBN to borrow more cheaply, even as it exceeded its initial offer for that maturity. * Deep Lore / Context: This auction result indicates a significant shift in investor sentiment and strategy within Nigeria's fixed-income market. The overwhelming preference for the 364-day T-bill suggests that investors are seeking to lock in current yields for a longer period, possibly anticipating future interest rate declines or seeking stability in a volatile economic environment. The CBN's ability to lower the borrowing rate for the most sought-after instrument, despite high demand, demonstrates its influence in managing government debt costs. This trend could provide support for a gradual decline in government borrowing costs and reinforce expectations of eventual interest rate cuts. * Deep Lore / Context: The CBN's monetary policy decisions, particularly concerning T-bill auctions, are crucial for managing liquidity, inflation, and government borrowing. The strong demand for longer-dated securities can be interpreted in several ways: it might reflect investor confidence in the stability of the Naira over the medium term, a strategic move to hedge against potential future inflation, or simply an attractive yield proposition compared to other investment options. The contrast with the subdued demand for shorter-term bills suggests a market that is looking beyond immediate returns. This dynamic is important for businesses seeking access to credit, as government borrowing costs can influence overall interest rate levels in the economy. * Source: Punch

Breaking Claude Code Opus 5 Auto Mode: Security Vulnerabilities Exposed Johann Rehberger, a prominent prompt injection researcher, has identified a significant vulnerability in Anthropic's Claude Code Opus 5 auto mode. Rehberger claims an attack can succeed 80% of the time by tricking Claude Code into downloading and uncompressing a zip archive, then executing code that imports base64 without recognizing it will execute a local struct.py file. In some instances, the auto mode even prevented the agent from terminating the malicious code. Rehberger strongly advises running unattended coding agents in sandboxed environments with restricted network egress and vigilant monitoring. * Deep Lore / Context: This finding directly addresses the "AI Security Vulnerabilities" storyline, demonstrating that even advanced AI safety features like Anthropic's auto mode can be bypassed. The attack vector, involving zip archives and base64 encoding, is sophisticated and exploits the AI's code execution capabilities. The fact that the auto mode not only failed to prevent the attack but also blocked the agent's attempts to clean up the compromise is particularly concerning. This reinforces the need for extreme caution when deploying AI agents, especially in sensitive environments. * Deep Lore / Context: The recommendation to use sandboxed environments, restrict network access, and monitor agents is a critical takeaway for anyone working with AI agents. It highlights the ongoing arms race between AI developers and malicious actors, where new vulnerabilities are constantly being discovered and exploited. The incident underscores the inherent risks associated with AI agents that can download, uncompress, and execute code. This development is particularly relevant for developers building AI-powered tools or integrating AI agents into their workflows, emphasizing that current safety measures are not foolproof. * Source: Simon Willison (AI)
