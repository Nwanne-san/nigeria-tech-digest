### FG Seeks Private Capital to Bridge Nigeria’s Gas Infrastructure Gap
**Source:** BusinessDay

**What happened:** The federal government is actively seeking increased private and development-finance investment to address the significant funding gaps in Nigeria’s gas infrastructure. At the 4th edition of the Gas Investment Forum on Tuesday, Ekperikpe Ekpo, the Minister of State for Petroleum Resources (Gas), represented by Shettima Gaji, Deputy Director of Gas Pricing and Open Access, explicitly stated that the government alone cannot finance the extensive infrastructure and projects required for the gas industry's development. He emphasized the critical need for "intentional partnerships" to attract domestic and international investors, financial institutions, development finance institutions, and technology providers to deliver "bankable projects" across the gas value chain. Public-private partnerships are deemed essential for bridging financing gaps, improving project preparation, and moving gas projects from concept to execution. Key projects identified include the Escravos-Lagos Pipeline System (ELPS) and the OB3 gas pipeline for domestic transmission, and regional initiatives like the West African Gas Pipeline, Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline, and Trans-Atlantic Gas Pipeline.

**Deep Lore / Context:** This is a crucial update to our ongoing storyline **"National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)"** (status: escalating), which has consistently highlighted the nation's economic challenges and the importance of revenue streams like NNPC profits. It also implicitly connects to **"CBN Policy and Economic Management"** (status: escalating) as robust infrastructure is foundational for economic stability and growth. Nigeria boasts Africa's largest proven gas reserves, estimated at over 200 trillion cubic feet, yet it has struggled to fully monetize this resource due to inadequate infrastructure for processing, transportation, and distribution. This "gas infrastructure gap" has historically constrained domestic power generation, industrial growth, and the country's potential as a major gas exporter.

The call for private capital is not new, but its renewed emphasis at a dedicated Gas Investment Forum underscores the government's recognition of the urgency and scale of investment required. The identified projects, particularly the ELPS and OB3, are vital for strengthening domestic gas supply to power plants and industries, which are critical for economic diversification and job creation. Regional projects like the Trans-Saharan and Trans-Atlantic pipelines aim to position Nigeria as a regional energy hub, facilitating cross-border trade and energy cooperation across Africa. However, attracting significant long-term private capital requires a stable regulatory environment, predictable policy frameworks, and a clear commitment to sanctity of contracts – factors that have sometimes been inconsistent in Nigeria's oil and gas sector. This initiative is a litmus test for the government's ability to create an investment-friendly climate and unlock Nigeria's vast gas potential for broader economic development and energy security.
