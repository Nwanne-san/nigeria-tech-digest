1. 2027: Rep Urges Single-Day Poll, Seeks Amendment of Electoral Act

Source: Punch

What happened: The Deputy Minority Leader of the House of Representatives, Abdussamad Dasuki, has called for a significant overhaul of Nigeria's electoral process, advocating for a single-day general election in 2027. This proposal aims to combine federal and state elections – covering presidential, senatorial, House of Representatives, governorship, and State House of Assembly polls – into one event, ideally in January or February 2027. Dasuki argued that this consolidation would lead to substantial economic savings by reducing logistical costs and allowances for ad hoc personnel and security agencies. Furthermore, he raised serious concerns about Section 63 (2) of the Electoral Act, which he warned could compromise the integrity of the 2027 elections by potentially allowing ballot papers without the prescribed official mark of the electoral commission to be counted under certain circumstances. He stressed that this provision creates a loophole for fraud and must be expunged, assuring that minority leaders are working to correct it.

Deep Lore / Context: This issue directly continues the "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" storyline, which has been escalating with persistent concerns over electoral violence, allegations of anti-graft reports being rejected, and a general erosion of public trust in the system. The call for a single-day election is a recurring debate in Nigeria's political discourse, often championed as a pragmatic solution to reduce the logistical complexities, security risks, and financial drain associated with staggered polls. Past elections have frequently been marred by violence, ballot box snatching, and other irregularities, which critics argue are exacerbated by the extended election periods that stretch security forces and create prolonged periods of political tension. The proposed amendment to Section 63(2) of the Electoral Act is a critical legislative effort to close a potential loophole that could facilitate electoral fraud, a persistent challenge that undermines democratic legitimacy.

The lawmaker's argument for cost savings resonates deeply in a nation grappling with "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" concerns, including public debt and the urgent need for prudent financial management. Reducing election expenses would free up vital funds for other critical social and economic development initiatives. Moreover, the emphasis on security directly addresses the "Nigeria's Infrastructure for Insecurity Response" storyline, acknowledging the pervasive insecurity across the country. This legislative push highlights an ongoing effort within the political class to refine Nigeria's electoral framework, even as "Political Integrity and Electoral Processes (Nigeria)" continues to be a source of public concern and scrutiny, both domestically and internationally.

2. FG Cracks Down on Unapproved Textbooks, Adopts Merit-Based Instructional Materials

Source: BusinessDay

What happened: The Federal Government, through the Minister of Education Tunji Alausa, has announced a significant intensification of efforts to improve the quality of instructional materials in Nigerian schools. Stricter measures against the use of unapproved textbooks will come into effect from September 2026, applying to all public primary, junior secondary, and senior secondary schools. A new National Textbook Ranking System will be introduced, establishing a merit-based and transparent process for evaluating and selecting learning resources. As part of this reform, schools, publishers, and authors found using or distributing unapproved materials in public schools will face blacklisting. To ease the financial burden on the publishing industry, the government has reduced the assessment fee for publishers from N2,000 to N1,500 per page, and the ranking fee per title from N1 million to N750,000, with provisions for refunds to those who paid under the old structure. Approved textbooks will hold validity for a minimum of three years, ensuring curriculum stability.

Deep Lore / Context: This policy is a significant step in the "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storyline, specifically focusing on improving the quality of education, which is a critical component of human capital development and long-term economic growth. While the previous digest mentioned "healthcare infrastructure receives boosts," this move extends the focus on foundational public services to the education sector. For many years, the Nigerian education system has grappled with pervasive issues of substandard instructional materials, which directly impact learning outcomes, contribute to poor academic performance, and ultimately diminish the quality of graduates entering the workforce. The introduction of a "merit-based and transparent process" aims to address long-standing concerns about corruption, favoritism, and the proliferation of low-quality textbooks in schools, which have often been driven by commercial interests rather than pedagogical quality.

The crackdown on unapproved textbooks and the establishment of a National Textbook Ranking and Selection Committee signal a serious and concerted commitment to strengthening curriculum delivery and rebuilding public trust in the education system. By capping the number of approved titles per subject (between six and ten) and requiring a minimum 70% score in the evaluation process, the government is setting clear and rigorous quality benchmarks.
