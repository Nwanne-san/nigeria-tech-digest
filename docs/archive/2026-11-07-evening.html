Banks Park N8.31 Trillion at CBN Despite N724 Billion OMO Mop-up

Source: Nairametrics

What happened Banking system liquidity remained substantial in the week ending November 6, 2026, with Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) balances at the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) soaring to N8.311 trillion. This significant liquidity persisted despite the CBN's efforts to mop up N724.32 billion at its Open Market Operations (OMO) auction on Friday, November 6. The SDF balances increased by N347.44 billion (4.36%) from N7.964 trillion on Thursday and N1.345 trillion (19.30%) from N6.966 trillion on Wednesday. Banks' opening balances, conversely, fell sharply by N417.96 billion (68.29%) between November 4 and November 6, indicating a shift of cash to the SDF. The CBN allotted N724.32 billion, representing 72.43% of the N1 trillion offered, accepting 38.37% of the N1.887 trillion in bids, with the 270-day bill drawing stronger demand but having a larger unallocated balance.

Deep Lore / Context This report is a critical update on the "CBN Policy and Economic Management" storyline, which we've been tracking as escalating, particularly concerning liquidity and FX volatility. The persistent high liquidity in the banking system, evidenced by the N8.311 trillion parked at the CBN's SDF, signals a significant challenge for the apex bank's monetary policy. The SDF is typically a facility where banks deposit excess funds, and such high balances suggest that commercial banks are finding it difficult to lend these funds productively in the real economy, or that attractive investment opportunities are scarce. This situation can be indicative of a weak credit demand environment or an abundance of funds that the real sector cannot absorb, potentially contributing to inflationary pressures if not effectively managed. The CBN's OMO auctions are a tool to mop up this excess liquidity and influence interest rates, but the latest auction's limited effect underscores the scale of the challenge.

The selective allotment at the OMO auction, especially for the longer-tenor 270-day bill despite strong demand, suggests the CBN is carefully managing its debt obligations and interest rate expectations. The fact that the N724.32 billion mop-up is roughly a quarter of the N2.95 trillion allotted just days earlier on November 3 further highlights the continuous battle against excess liquidity. This ongoing struggle to manage banking system liquidity has direct implications for the "National Progress & Economy (Nigeria)" storyline, as it affects the cost of borrowing, investment climate, and ultimately, economic growth. The Financial Markets Dealers Association (FMDA) projects further inflows of N13.79 trillion in November, driven by OMO maturities, FAAC allocations, and Treasury Bills repayments. This outlook suggests that the CBN will face continued pressure to intensify its mop-up operations, and the upcoming Monetary Policy Committee meeting will be crucial in determining the next steps to address this liquidity overhang and its impact on the naira and interest rates.

AI Broke the Cybersecurity World. What’s Next?

Source: The Verge

What happened According to Alex Stamos, Chief Security Officer at Cognition and a long-time cybersecurity expert, AI has fundamentally disrupted the cybersecurity industry, pushing it into a "dark age." Stamos states that the multibillion-dollar industry is "overdue to the unrelenting, AI-boosted waves of hacks, breaches, and attacks." Both private and state actors, including Russia and North Korea, are leveraging AI to automate most of the "kill chain" – from detecting vulnerabilities to writing and deploying exploits in real-time. He highlighted the Chinese open-source tool ARTEX, which, when powered by models like an "obliterated GLM-5.3," can break into a "shocking number of organizations," including three South Korean banks and 200 more companies this week. Stamos likened the current situation to the "Wild West days" of the 1990s, but with much greater automation and economic incentive for attackers.

Deep Lore / Context This report directly and significantly escalates our "Adversarial AI Evolution and Cybersecurity" storyline. We've been tracking how AI accelerates cyber threats and introduces new attack vectors, and Stamos's assessment confirms a dramatic shift. The core issue is that AI is democratizing sophisticated hacking capabilities. Previously, finding vulnerabilities and writing exploits required specialized human expertise and significant time. Now, open-weight AI models and tools like ARTEX can automate these steps, allowing adversaries to scale attacks at machine speed. This inversion of the "kill chain" means that defense mechanisms, traditionally built around human-speed detection and response, are becoming obsolete. The "permanent continuous breach" described in the tech industry, affecting "AI-related companies, the neoclouds," and even major financial platforms like Binance (the "largest heist of anything in human history"), underscores the severity of this new era.

The implications are profound. The established "economy of cyber," which included bug bounties and an etiquette around vulnerability disclosure, is under threat. The ability to "just download an open-source tool and break into multi-billion dollar companies" means the barrier to entry for cybercriminals has drastically lowered. This creates a much larger ecosystem of attackers, many operating from jurisdictions beyond the reach of law enforcement, driven by significant economic incentives. The comparison to the 1990s "Wild West" is apt, but with a critical difference: the entire world now runs on the internet, making the potential for global disruption far greater. Cloud providers are developing machine-speed defense mechanisms, but the rapid evolution of offensive AI capabilities suggests a continuous arms race, demanding a fundamental rethinking of cybersecurity strategies and the development of verifiable AI solutions.

Why AWS Wants Developers to Stop Babysitting Their AI Agents

Source: The New Stack

What happened AWS has introduced Kiro bots, an enhancement to its agentic, spec-driven development environment, Kiro. First launched in July 2025, Kiro now features persistent agent memory and delegated workflow management, aiming to free developers from "babysitting" their AI agents. Kathy Kam, AWS Agentic AI director of engineering, explained that Kiro bots are "named, long-running agent[s] that you define and customize for a specific part of your work," each retaining its own memory and starting with only the tools provided at creation. This allows developers to delegate entire processes, not just tasks, with bots launching and monitoring parallel worker sessions. Kiro bots prevent "context rot" by maintaining durable memory, and their permissions are strictly controlled by developers, who remain accountable while the agents are responsible for execution.

Deep Lore / Context This development is a significant acceleration of the "AI Model Competition and Enterprise Adoption" storyline, which we've been tracking as escalating. AWS's Kiro bots represent a crucial step towards more autonomous and reliable AI agents in enterprise development workflows. The concept of "agentic systems" is at the forefront of AI innovation, aiming to move beyond single-turn prompts to systems that can plan, execute, and adapt over longer periods. The challenge of "context rot" – where agents lose track of previous interactions or parallel tasks – has been a major hurdle in making these systems truly useful for complex engineering tasks.
